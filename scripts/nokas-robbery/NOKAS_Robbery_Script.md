# THE GLASS — The NOKAS Robbery
### Animated documentary script · Runtime: ~13:00 · One narrator · 2D animation with four 3D cinematic sequences

**Format notes**
- **NARRATOR**: one voice from start to finish. Calm, low and controlled. They never shout, and the tension comes from restraint.
- **VISUAL** lines are directions for the animation team. **SFX / MUSIC** are cues for the sound designer.
- Pacing assumes about 140 words per minute of narration plus pauses. Timestamps are targets.
- **Red thread: the glass.** One bulletproof window runs through the whole story. It beat the gang twice, and the third time it broke. Everything after that point also breaks: the plan, the getaway and the gang. Bring the window back visually in every chapter, as a reflection, a crack or a shard.
- **Second motif: the clock.** 5 April 2004 opens the story, and 5 April 2005 closes it.
- **Tone:** a police officer died in this robbery. Make it thrilling, but do not glorify it. Show no gore, and keep the robbers as silhouettes or masked figures. Arne Sigve Klungland is the one person shown with warmth.

### Two visual worlds: 2D and 3D

The video is **2D animation** throughout. It switches to **3D cinematic animation** for four one-minute sequences at the turning points of the story. These run *inside* the existing timeline, so the total runtime does not change.

| # | 3D cinematic | Timecode | Chapter | What it shows |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 🎬 1 | **The Window Wins** | 3:45 – 4:45 | Ch. 3 | The earlier, failed attempt on the window, at night |
| 🎬 2 | **08:00** | 6:00 – 7:00 | Ch. 4 | Both fronts at once: the burning police garage and the window breaking |
| 🎬 3 | **The Officer** | 8:00 – 9:00 | Ch. 5 | Klungland's drive toward the depot, told with restraint |
| 🎬 4 | **One Year to the Day** | 10:15 – 11:15 | Ch. 7 | Toska's arrest in Málaga, 5 April 2005 |

**Rules for the 3D sequences**
- **Enter and exit through glass.** Every 3D sequence starts when the 2D frame cracks and shatters into 3D space. It ends when the 3D image is caught in a pane of glass, a reflection or a windshield, which flattens back into 2D. This makes the red thread the doorway between the two worlds.
- **Narration is sparse.** Use about 40 to 70 words per minute, compared with about 140 in 2D. The pictures carry the story, and the narrator only steers.
- **Look:** grounded and photographic in its lighting, but stylised rather than photoreal. Use a slightly desaturated palette, volumetric light, practical light sources and shallow depth of field. The cameras should feel physical, using dolly, handheld and crane moves, with no impossible moves.
- **Characters** match the 2D designs in proportion and silhouette, so viewers recognise them across the switch. Robbers stay masked and faceless.
- **Sound** becomes fully spatial: the score drops away and room tone, wind and distance take over.

## 0:00 – 0:45 · COLD OPEN *(2D)*

**VISUAL:** Pure black. Then one hairline crack appears across the screen, as if the viewer is looking through glass.

**SFX:** A single sledgehammer strike on armoured glass. Deep and dull, with no echo. Then total silence.

**NARRATOR** *(00:01)*:
> At eight o'clock on a Monday morning, in one of the safest cities in the world…
> the police could not get out of their own police station.

**SFX:** A truck engine roars, then the *whoomp* of fire catching.

**VISUAL:** Quick cut to a stylised police garage ramp. A white truck sits across the exit and bursts into flames. Orange light floods the frame.

**NARRATOR:**
> Their exit was burning. Spikes were spread across the roads. Smoke drifted past the front door.
> And a short drive away, in the shadow of a 900-year-old cathedral, men in black were firing into a window.

**SFX:** Automatic fire, muffled as if heard from inside a building. It keeps going and does not stop.

**NARRATOR:**
> They fired one hundred and thirteen bullets into one window.
> This is the story of that window. Of the men who spent a year trying to break it.
> And of what it cost when they finally did.

**VISUAL:** The crack spreads across the whole screen and the glass SHATTERS toward the camera. Each shard reflects a different face that we will meet later: a ringleader, a sledgehammer, a policeman, a seafront in Spain.

**MUSIC:** A hard hit, then the title.

## 0:45 – 2:00 · CHAPTER 1 — THE CITY OF MONEY *(2D)*

**VISUAL:** Aerial animation of Stavanger at dawn: white wooden houses, the harbour, offshore supply ships. Oil platforms glow on the horizon like distant cities.

**NARRATOR:**
> Stavanger, on Norway's west coast. A small city that struck it rich.
> Since the 1970s, oil from the North Sea has made it one of the wealthiest places on the planet.
> Where there is money, there is cash. And cash has to be counted somewhere.

**VISUAL:** Push in through the old town to the cathedral square. A plain, solid building on Kongens gate. A sign reads **NOKAS – Norsk Kontantservice**.

**NARRATOR:**
> That somewhere was NOKAS, Norway's cash-handling service.
> Behind these walls, millions of kroner moved every day. They were counted, sorted, sealed and sent out to banks across the region.
> The building sat in the middle of town, by the cathedral, the shops and a busy pedestrian street.
> Hiding in plain sight was its security. Nobody would dare rob a vault in the middle of the city.

**VISUAL:** Inside, we follow a cash cart past a thick window that looks out on a back courtyard. Hold on the glass. It is greenish and layered, and it reflects the room.

**NARRATOR:**
> The building's real defence wasn't a gun or a guard.
> It was glass. Armoured, layered, and built to take punishment.
> Remember this window.

## 2:00 – 3:30 · CHAPTER 2 — THE ARCHITECT *(2D)*

**VISUAL:** Shift to Oslo, in a colder palette. A silhouette at a kitchen table covered in maps, notebooks and a stopwatch.

**NARRATOR:**
> Five hundred kilometres east, in Oslo, a man was making a plan.
> His name was David Toska.
> He was not the kind of criminal who kicks in a door and hopes for the best. Toska was a planner.
> He thought in timelines, routes and minutes.

**VISUAL:** Toska's notebook pages animate. Arrows, clocks and the outline of a building appear, and in the centre a rectangle labelled *VINDU*, Norwegian for "window".

**NARRATOR:**
> His target was the NOKAS depot in Stavanger.
> The money was guarded by a building, and a building can be beaten.
> The police, however, can always come. Unless they can't.

**VISUAL:** The notebook splits into two columns: **GET IN** and **KEEP THEM OUT**.

**NARRATOR:**
> So the plan had two halves.
> One team would break into the depot.
> The other team's job was to freeze an entire city's police force in place, for just long enough.

**VISUAL:** Masked silhouettes step forward one by one, each tagged with a role: *DRIVER, BLOCKER, SPOTTER, GUNMAN, SLEDGEHAMMER.*

**NARRATOR:**
> He built a crew of more than a dozen men.
> Among them was Erling Havnå, later known as *the sledgehammer man*.
> There was Metkel Betew, one of the central figures.
> And there was Kjell Alrich Schumann, a name that would come to matter more than any other in this story.

**MUSIC:** A low pulse starts, like a heartbeat.

## 3:30 – 5:15 · CHAPTER 3 — STRIKE ONE, STRIKE TWO

### 3:30 – 3:45 · *(2D)*

**VISUAL:** Toska's notebook. The *VINDU* rectangle is circled again and again until the pen tears the paper.

**NARRATOR:**
> Here is the part most people don't know.
> The robbery on the fifth of April was not the first attempt. It was the third.

**TRANSITION → 3D:** The torn paper becomes a crack, the crack becomes glass, and the glass shatters into depth.

### 🎬 3:45 – 4:45 · 3D CINEMATIC 1 — "THE WINDOW WINS"

*Mood: silent, cold and humiliating. A heist that fails before it begins.*

| Time | Shot | Camera | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:10 | Night, the NOKAS back courtyard in a light winter drizzle. A sodium streetlight buzzes. Raindrops slide down the armoured window. | Slow crane down from the rooftops to window height. | Rain, a distant car, the streetlight hum. No music. |
| 0:10–0:20 | Three masked figures move along the wall in single file. One carries a sledgehammer. Their breath steams. | Low handheld, tracking behind them at knee height. | Footsteps on wet stone, nylon rustling. |
| 0:20–0:35 | The sledgehammer swings. In slow motion it hits the glass, which flexes and ripples like water and then **holds**. Only a small white star appears. | Extreme close-up on the glass from the inside, so the hammer comes at the camera. | A deep, dull *THUNK*. Then the sound drops out completely. |
| 0:35–0:45 | Again. And again. The figures stop. One puts a gloved hand flat on the glass. | Over-the-shoulder, with the reflection of the masked face in the window. | Heavy breathing, rain. |
| 0:45–0:60 | They retreat into the dark. The camera stays on the window. The small white star catches the streetlight. | A slow push in until the star fills the frame. | One low cello note rises. |

**NARRATOR** *(sparse, under 0:20–0:60)*:
> The window won.
> The armoured glass was far stronger than Toska had calculated.
> A plan built to the minute… stopped by a pane of glass.
> *(pause)*
> Most crews would have walked away.

**TRANSITION → 2D:** The white star on the glass flattens into a 2D tally mark in Toska's notebook.

### 4:45 – 5:15 · *(2D)*

**VISUAL:** Late winter. Headlights wind through dark roads out of the city into a forest marked **SØRMARKA**.

**NARRATOR:**
> Toska went back to the drawing board. More force. More time.
> All through the late winter of 2004, the gang test-drove their escape route over and over, from the city centre out to the woods at Sørmarka.
> By April, they knew Stavanger's streets better than the people whose job was to protect them.

**VISUAL:** In Toska's notebook, a third tally mark appears beside *VINDU*.

## 5:15 – 7:45 · CHAPTER 4 — 5 APRIL 2004

### 5:15 – 6:00 · *(2D)*

**VISUAL:** Title flash: **MONDAY, 5 APRIL 2004 · 03:00**. Darkness. Vans and trucks move into position in silence.

**NARRATOR:**
> In the early hours of Monday the fifth of April, the pieces moved onto the board.
> Weapons, spike strips and smoke grenades were all accounted for.

**VISUAL:** Clock: **07:59**. Ordinary life. A man with a coffee, a cyclist, shop shutters rolling up on the cathedral square.

**NARRATOR:**
> Stavanger is waking up. People are heading to work, and the shops are opening.
> None of them know they are standing on the board.

**MUSIC:** It drops to near silence. Just a ticking clock.

**VISUAL:** The clock face fills the screen. Its glass cover cracks at **08:00**.

**TRANSITION → 3D:** The clock glass shatters and we fall through it.

### 🎬 6:00 – 7:00 · 3D CINEMATIC 2 — "08:00"

*Mood: chaos, controlled by the edit. Two fronts that cut together faster and faster until they lock in sync.*

| Time | Shot | Camera | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:08 | **FRONT ONE.** The police HQ on Lagårdsveien. A white truck rolls down the garage ramp and stops dead across the exit. | Static wide security-camera angle, then a hard cut to ground level. | Air brakes hiss. |
| 0:08–0:15 | A masked figure walks away. Behind him, the truck ignites, and fire fills the ramp in a single breath. | A dolly back in front of the figure as he walks toward the camera, with the fire blooming behind. | A *WHOOMP*, then the roar of flame. |
| 0:15–0:22 | Tear-gas canisters bounce across the front steps of the station. Smoke rolls against the glass doors. Inside, blurred uniforms are trapped. | From inside the lobby, looking out through the doors. | Hissing canisters, muffled shouting. |
| 0:22–0:28 | A spike strip unrolls across an empty street. | A low-angle macro shot as the spikes pass close to the lens. | Metallic clatter. |

### 7:00 – 7:45 · *(2D)*

**VISUAL:** Inside the counting room, from a low angle. Staff are pressed against the walls, frightened faces lit by flashes. Masked figures come through the hole.

**NARRATOR:**
> Inside, NOKAS employees watch armed men climb into their workplace.
> The robbers demand the money, and they get it: bag after bag, more than **57 million kroner**.

## 7:45 – 9:05 · CHAPTER 5 — THE OFFICER

### 7:45 – 8:00 · *(2D)*

**MUSIC:** Everything stops. A single sustained piano note.

**VISUAL:** Softer, warmer animation. A portrait of a man in uniform: an ordinary, decent face.

**NARRATOR:**

### 🎬 8:00 – 9:00 · 3D CINEMATIC 3 — "THE OFFICER"

*Mood: dignity and dread. This is the most restrained sequence in the film. Show no violence on screen, no blood and no impact. The audience understands through the glass and through the silence.*

|---|---|---|---|

| 0:15–0:25 | The vehicle turns into the streets near NOKAS. In the distance, smoke rises. | From the windshield's point of view, a slow approach. | The radio grows louder and more urgent, then distorts. |
| 0:25–0:35 | At a corner far ahead, a masked figure, a dark silhouette, raises a rifle. | The windshield's point of view. A rack focus pulls from the glass to the figure. | Total silence. Every sound is cut. |
| 0:35–0:40 | The windshield cracks into a spider web, three times. *The glass again.* | Locked on the windshield. We never see beyond it. | Three muffled shots, as if through water. Then a high-pitched ringing. |

## 9:05 – 9:45 · CHAPTER 6 — INTO THE WOODS *(2D)*

**MUSIC:** The pulse returns, faster now.

**VISUAL:** A top-down map of Stavanger. Red lines race from the city centre outward along the rehearsed route, then vanish one by one into the dark green forest of Sørmarka.

**NARRATOR:**

## 9:45 – 11:15 · CHAPTER 7 — THE HUNT

### 9:45 – 10:15 · *(2D)*

**VISUAL:** An investigation board in the style of a crime wall: photos, red string and phone records. Faces get an X, one after another. A brief inset in a painted style shows Munch's *The Scream* hanging in a museum, then an empty frame.

**VISUAL:** Toska's photo on the board, under glass. Sunlight from a window glares across it, washing it white.

## 12:15 – 13:00 · EPILOGUE — WHAT THE GLASS KEPT *(2D)*

**VISUAL:** Back to the NOKAS courtyard, years later. Daylight. The window has been repaired and is perfect again. Our reflection appears in it.

**NARRATOR:**
> And the money?
> More than fifty-seven million kroner was taken that morning.
> Only around six million has ever been accounted for.
> The rest, roughly fifty million kroner, has never been found.

**VISUAL:** Slow push in on the glass. In the reflection, quick flashes play back in their original 3D look: the white star, the burning truck, the cracked windshield and the Málaga car window.

**NARRATOR:**
> The plan took more than a year. The robbery took minutes.
> The glass held them back twice, and the third time it broke.
> Behind it, they found more money than anyone had ever stolen in Norway.
> They also lost everything else.

**VISUAL:** The reflection fades until only the cathedral square remains, with ordinary people walking through the morning.

**NARRATOR** *(final line)*:
> Somewhere out there, fifty million kroner is still missing.
> And somewhere in Stavanger, a family still remembers the Monday he went to work… and drove toward the gunfire.

**VISUAL:** Black. Text: *In memory of Arne Sigve Klungland.*

## Production notes

**Runtime:** about 13:00. That is 9 minutes of 2D and 4 minutes of 3D cinematic. The four 3D minutes replace 2D minutes that were already in the timeline, and the narration around them was tightened, so the video is not longer than before. The narration is now about 1,100 words, because the 3D minutes carry far fewer words. The rest of the time goes to pauses, music beats and the 3D minutes, which are mostly picture.

**3D production priorities**, from the most to the least demanding:
1. **🎬 2 "08:00"** has two locations, fire, smoke, slow-motion glass and the most shots. Budget it first.
2. **🎬 4 "One Year to the Day"** needs an exterior seafront environment and a small crowd.
3. **🎬 1 "The Window Wins"** needs one courtyard set, rain and glass simulation. The courtyard and window can be **reused in 🎬 2**, relit for daylight, which saves build time.
4. **🎬 3 "The Officer"** needs one vehicle interior and one street. It is the simplest in polygons but the hardest in tone. Test it with an audience before final render.

**Shared 3D assets:** the NOKAS courtyard and window (🎬 1 and 2), the glass-crack shader (all four), the masked robber rig (🎬 1, 2 and 3), the police vehicle (🎬 3) and the cathedral spire (🎬 3, and as a backdrop in 🎬 2).

**Recurring visual motifs (the red thread):**
1. **The glass.** It appears in the opening crack, the courtyard window, the windshield of the police car, the car window in Málaga and the reflection in the epilogue. Glass is also the doorway every time the film passes between 2D and 3D.
2. **The clock.** It starts at 03:00 and 08:00 on 5 April 2004, and it closes on 5 April 2005.
3. **The hammer sound.** It is the first sound of the film, it hits the window in 🎬 1 and 🎬 2, and it returns as the car door in 🎬 4.

**Fact-check before publishing.** These points were confirmed in search results:
- The date (5 April 2004, about 08:00) and the place (NOKAS counting centre, Kongens gate, Stavanger city centre).
- The police HQ on Lagårdsveien was blocked by a burning truck at the garage exit, with tear gas at the entrance and spike strips on the roads.
- Entry was through the courtyard window, using a sledgehammer and a battering ram, with 113 shots fired into it.
- The robbery was the third attempt, and the glass had beaten an earlier one.
- The escape route to Sørmarka was test-driven in late winter 2004.
- About 57.4 million NOK was stolen. About 6 million has been accounted for, and about 51 million was never recovered.
- Police lieutenant Arne Sigve Klungland was killed by a shot to the head while in a police command vehicle.
- Thirteen men were convicted on 10 March 2006, with 181 years in total.
- Kjell Alrich Schumann admitted firing the fatal shots during the Gulating appeal in September 2006.
- Toska confessed on 12 April 2005 but denied shooting Klungland. He fled to Spain with his partner and their one-year-old son, and was arrested in Málaga on 5 April 2005 after surveillance and email tracing.
- Erling Havnå was known as "the sledgehammer man" (*sleggemannen*), and Metkel Betew was among those convicted.

**Dramatised details in the 3D sequences.** These are staging choices, not documented facts. Keep them atmospheric, or verify them first:
- 🎬 1: the rain, the night and the exact way the earlier attempt played out. It is documented only that the glass defeated an earlier attempt.
- 🎬 2: the robber walking away from the truck, and the exact choreography.
- 🎬 3: the radio chatter, the route, the number of shots and the windshield breaking. The documented facts are only that he was in a police command vehicle and was shot in the head.
- 🎬 4: the internet café, the stakeout car, the coffee cup and the arrest choreography. The documented facts are only the arrest in Málaga on 5 April 2005, after surveillance and email tracing.

**Verify or soften these points before publishing:**
- *The exact number of robbers at the scene.* The script says "more than a dozen" to stay safe.
- *Toska's final sentence.* Sources disagree between 18, 19 and 21 years, so the script deliberately gives no figure.
- *The link to the theft of The Scream.* The script presents it only as a theory that was never proven, and it should stay that way.
- *"Biggest robbery in Norwegian history".* This was true at the time, but it was later surpassed, so keep the words "at the time".
- *The family in the final line.* Check before naming any relatives of Klungland.
