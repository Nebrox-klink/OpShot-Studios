# THE GLASS — The NOKAS Robbery
### Animated documentary script · Runtime target: ~12–13 minutes · One narrator

**Format notes**
- **NARRATOR**: one voice from start to finish. Calm, low and controlled. They never shout, and the tension comes from restraint.
- **VISUAL** lines are directions for the animation team. **SFX / MUSIC** are cues for the sound designer.
- Pacing assumes about 140 words per minute of narration plus pauses. Timestamps are targets.
- **Red thread: the glass.** One bulletproof window runs through the whole story. It beat the gang twice, and the third time it broke. Everything after that point also breaks: the plan, the getaway and the gang. Bring the window back visually in every chapter, as a reflection, a crack or a shard.
- **Second motif: the clock.** 5 April 2004 opens the story, and 5 April 2005 closes it.
- **Tone:** a police officer died in this robbery. Make it thrilling, but do not glorify it. Show no gore, and keep the robbers as silhouettes or masked figures. Arne Sigve Klungland is the one person shown with warmth.

---

## 0:00 – 0:45 · COLD OPEN

**VISUAL:** Pure black. Then one hairline crack appears across the screen, as if the viewer is looking through glass.

**SFX:** A single sledgehammer strike on armoured glass. Deep and dull, with no echo. Then total silence.

**NARRATOR** *(00:01)*:
> At eight o'clock on a Monday morning, in one of the safest cities in the world…
> the police could not get out of their own police station.

**SFX:** A truck engine roars, then the *whoomp* of fire catching.

**VISUAL:** Quick cut to a stylised police garage ramp. A white truck sits across the exit and bursts into flames. Orange light floods the frame.

**NARRATOR:**
> Their exit was burning. Spikes were spread across the roads. Smoke drifted past the front door.
> And six hundred metres away, a few steps from a 900-year-old cathedral, men in black were firing into a window.

**SFX:** Automatic fire, muffled as if heard from inside a building. It keeps going and does not stop.

**NARRATOR:**
> They fired one hundred and thirteen bullets into one window.
> This is the story of that window. Of the men who spent a year trying to break it.
> And of what it cost when they finally did.

**VISUAL:** The crack spreads across the whole screen and the glass SHATTERS toward the camera. Each shard reflects a different face that we will meet later: a ringleader, a sledgehammer, a policeman, a child in a stroller.

**MUSIC:** A hard hit, then the title.

### TITLE CARD: **THE GLASS — The NOKAS Robbery · Stavanger, 5 April 2004**

---

## 0:45 – 2:00 · CHAPTER 1 — THE CITY OF MONEY

**VISUAL:** Aerial animation of Stavanger at dawn: white wooden houses, the harbour, offshore supply ships. Oil platforms glow on the horizon like distant cities.

**NARRATOR:**
> Stavanger, on Norway's west coast. A small city that struck it rich.
> Since the 1970s, oil from the North Sea has made it one of the wealthiest places on the planet.
> Where there is money, there is cash. And cash has to be counted somewhere.

**VISUAL:** Push in through the old town to the cathedral square. A plain, solid building on Kongens gate. A sign reads **NOKAS – Norsk Kontantservice**.

**NARRATOR:**
> That somewhere was NOKAS, Norway's cash-handling service.
> Behind these walls, millions of kroner moved every day. They were counted, sorted, sealed and sent out to banks across the region.
> The building sat in the middle of town, by the cathedral, the shops and a busy pedestrian street.
> Hiding in plain sight was its security. Nobody would dare rob a vault in the middle of the city.

**VISUAL:** Inside, we follow a cash cart past a thick window that looks out on a back courtyard. Hold on the glass. It is greenish and layered, and it reflects the room.

**NARRATOR:**
> The building's real defence wasn't a gun or a guard.
> It was glass. Armoured, layered, and built to take punishment.
> Remember this window. It is the reason everything that follows happened the way it did.

---

## 2:00 – 3:45 · CHAPTER 2 — THE ARCHITECT

**VISUAL:** Shift to Oslo, in a colder palette. A silhouette at a kitchen table covered in maps, notebooks and a stopwatch.

**NARRATOR:**
> Five hundred kilometres east, in Oslo, a man was making a plan.
> His name was David Toska.
> He was not the kind of criminal who kicks in a door and hopes for the best. Toska was a planner.
> He thought in timelines, routes and minutes. He studied response times the way an engineer studies stress loads.

**VISUAL:** Toska's notebook pages animate. Arrows, clocks and the outline of a building appear, and in the centre a rectangle labelled *VINDU*, Norwegian for "window".

**NARRATOR:**
> His target was the NOKAS depot in Stavanger.
> His theory was simple: the money inside was guarded by a building, and a building can be beaten.
> The police, however, can always come. Unless they can't.

**VISUAL:** The notebook splits into two columns: **GET IN** and **KEEP THEM OUT**.

**NARRATOR:**
> So the plan had two halves.
> One team would break into the depot.
> The other team's job was to freeze an entire city's police force in place, for just long enough.

**VISUAL:** Masked silhouettes step forward one by one, each tagged with a role: *DRIVER, BLOCKER, SPOTTER, GUNMAN, SLEDGEHAMMER.*

**NARRATOR:**
> He built a crew of more than a dozen men.
> Among them was Erling Havnå, a former kickboxer who would later be known by a grim nickname: *the sledgehammer man*.
> There was Metkel Betew, who would be tried as one of the central figures.
> And there was Kjell Alrich Schumann, a name that would come to matter more than any other in this story.

**MUSIC:** A low pulse starts, like a heartbeat.

---

## 3:45 – 5:00 · CHAPTER 3 — STRIKE ONE, STRIKE TWO

**VISUAL:** Night. The back courtyard of NOKAS. Shadowy figures approach the window.

**NARRATOR:**
> Here is the part most people don't know.
> The robbery of the fifth of April was not the first attempt.
> It was the third.

**SFX:** A hammer strikes the glass with a dull *thunk*. Nothing happens.

**NARRATOR:**
> In earlier attempts, the gang went for that window, and the window won.
> The armoured glass was far stronger than Toska had calculated.
> The plan, which had been built to the minute, stopped at a pane of glass.

**VISUAL:** Rewind effect. The figures retreat, and calendar pages flip back and forth.

**NARRATOR:**
> Most crews would walk away. Toska went back to the drawing board.
> If the glass needed more time, he would buy more time.
> If it needed more force, he would bring more force.

**VISUAL:** Late winter. Headlights wind through dark roads out of the city into a forest marked **SØRMARKA**.

**NARRATOR:**
> In the late winter of 2004, the gang test-drove their escape route over and over, from the city centre out to the woods at Sørmarka.
> They learned every turn. They timed every stretch.
> By April, they believed they knew Stavanger's streets better than the people whose job was to protect them.

**VISUAL:** Toska's notebook. The rectangle labelled *VINDU* now has a third tally mark beside it.

---

## 5:00 – 7:45 · CHAPTER 4 — 5 APRIL 2004

**VISUAL:** Title flash: **MONDAY, 5 APRIL 2004 · 03:00**. Darkness. Vans and trucks move into position in silence.

**NARRATOR:**
> In the early hours of Monday the fifth of April, the pieces moved onto the board.
> Vehicles were placed at key points across the city. Weapons, spike strips and smoke grenades were all accounted for.

**VISUAL:** Clock: **07:59**. Ordinary life. A man with a coffee, a cyclist, a parent pushing a stroller across the cathedral square. Morning light.

**NARRATOR:**
> Stavanger is waking up. People are heading to work, the shops are opening, and the bells of the cathedral are about to ring.
> None of them know they are standing on the board.

**MUSIC:** It drops to near silence. Just a ticking clock.

**VISUAL:** **08:00.**

**SFX:** Everything at once: engines, glass and gunfire.

### Front one: the police station

**VISUAL:** Split-screen. On the left, the Stavanger police headquarters on Lagårdsveien. A white truck rolls down the ramp toward the garage exit, stops, and erupts in fire.

**NARRATOR:**
> Front one: the police headquarters.
> A truck drives down the ramp to the garage, is set on fire, and seals the vehicle exit.
> Tear gas is thrown at the main entrance. Spike strips are laid across the roads.
> In the span of a few moments, Stavanger's police are trapped behind their own doors.

### Front two: the window

**VISUAL:** Right side of the split-screen: the NOKAS courtyard. Masked men run at the window, one swinging a sledgehammer and others carrying a battering ram.

**NARRATOR:**
> Front two: the window.
> This time they came with a sledgehammer, a battering ram, and automatic weapons.

**SFX:** Hammer. Ram. Then gunfire into the glass, rhythmic and relentless.

**VISUAL:** Extreme close-up of the glass. Each bullet leaves a white star, and the stars multiply until the window is more crack than glass. A small counter in the corner ticks upward: 20 … 57 … 89 … **113**.

**NARRATOR:**
> One hundred and thirteen shots, into a single window.
> Inside, NOKAS employees hear the walls of their workplace being torn open around them.
> This is the third time this glass has been attacked. Now, finally, it gives way.

**SFX:** The glass gives. It is not a clean shatter but a sickening crunch.

**VISUAL:** Inside the counting room, from a low angle. Staff are pressed against the walls, frightened faces lit by muzzle flashes. Masked figures come through the hole.

**NARRATOR:**
> The robbers pour into the counting room.
> They demand the money, and they get it: bag after bag, more than **57 million kroner**.
> At the time, it was the biggest robbery in Norwegian history.

---

## 7:45 – 9:00 · CHAPTER 5 — THE OFFICER

**MUSIC:** Everything stops. A single sustained piano note.

**VISUAL:** Softer, warmer animation. A police vehicle drives toward the city centre. Inside is a man in uniform. We see him clearly: an ordinary, decent face.

**NARRATOR:**
> Not every police officer in Stavanger was trapped behind that burning truck.
> Police lieutenant Arne Sigve Klungland was out, in a police command vehicle, and he was heading toward the gunfire.

**VISUAL:** The vehicle turns into the street near NOKAS. From the windshield's point of view, we see a masked man at the corner raise a rifle.

**NARRATOR:**
> The gang had positioned men outside the depot to cover the robbery.
> When the police car came into view, they opened fire.

**SFX:** Three shots, muffled, as if heard through the car's windshield. Then a high-pitched ringing.

**VISUAL:** Do not show the impact. Show the windshield cracking into a spider web. *The glass again.* Cut to white.

**NARRATOR** *(slowly)*:
> One of the bullets struck Arne Sigve Klungland in the head.
> He died there, in the middle of his own city, on a Monday morning.

**VISUAL:** Hold on white for two full seconds. Then show a simple, respectful memorial frame: his silhouette, a single flower, the date.

**NARRATOR:**
> Remember his name. It is the reason this was never just a robbery.

---

## 9:00 – 9:45 · CHAPTER 6 — INTO THE WOODS

**MUSIC:** The pulse returns, faster now.

**VISUAL:** A top-down map of Stavanger. Red lines race from the city centre outward, exactly along the route they rehearsed.

**NARRATOR:**
> The robbers loaded the money and ran.
> Out of the centre, along the route they had driven again and again all winter, toward the forest at Sørmarka.
> Vehicles were swapped and evidence was burned.
> Within minutes, the men in black dissolved into the trees.

**VISUAL:** The red lines vanish one by one into a dark green forest. Silence.

**NARRATOR:**
> The plan had worked, almost perfectly.
> But Toska had planned to beat a window, a police station and a city.
> He had not planned to kill a police officer. And from this moment, Norway would not rest until every man was found.

---

## 9:45 – 11:15 · CHAPTER 7 — THE HUNT

**VISUAL:** An investigation board in the style of a crime wall. Photos, red string, phone records, bank slips.

**NARRATOR:**
> What followed became one of the largest criminal investigations in Norwegian history.
> Piece by piece, phone by phone, the network came apart.
> Arrests followed across the country.

**VISUAL:** A cutaway in a different style, painted and dreamlike. Munch's *The Scream* hangs in a museum, then the frame is suddenly empty.

**NARRATOR:**
> And in August that year, something strange happened in Oslo.
> Armed men walked into the Munch Museum and stole *The Scream*.
> Some wondered whether it was more than a coincidence. Was it a distraction, or a bargaining chip to take pressure off the NOKAS investigation?
> It has never been proven. But it shows how far the shadow of that Monday morning reached.

**VISUAL:** Return to the board. One photo is left without an X: **DAVID TOSKA.**

**NARRATOR:**
> The architect was still missing.
> Toska knew it was only a matter of time. So he took his partner and their one-year-old son, and he left for Spain.

**VISUAL:** Sunlit Costa del Sol. Palm trees and white apartment blocks. A man in sunglasses walks along a seafront. *The clock appears in the corner and runs fast.*

**NARRATOR:**
> Sun. Sea. A new life, and a long way from Stavanger.
> But the investigators were patient. They watched. They followed. And they traced his emails.

**VISUAL:** Clock stops: **5 APRIL 2005.**

**NARRATOR:**
> On the fifth of April 2005, Spanish police arrested David Toska in Málaga.
> It was one year to the day after the robbery.

**SFX:** A cell door slams. The sound echoes the hammer strike from the very first second of the film.

---

## 11:15 – 12:15 · CHAPTER 8 — THE VERDICT

**VISUAL:** A courtroom in Stavanger, in a muted palette. Rows of defendants are drawn as simple, faceless shapes.

**NARRATOR:**
> On the tenth of March 2006, thirteen men were convicted in Stavanger for the NOKAS robbery.
> Together, they were sentenced to a total of one hundred and eighty-one years in prison.
> David Toska confessed to the robbery, but denied firing the fatal shot.

**VISUAL:** One shape in the dock slowly gains definition.

**NARRATOR:**
> In September 2006, during the appeal at the Gulating Court of Appeal, one man finally admitted it.
> Kjell Alrich Schumann was the one who fired the shots that killed Arne Sigve Klungland.

---

## 12:15 – 13:00 · EPILOGUE — WHAT THE GLASS KEPT

**VISUAL:** Back to the NOKAS courtyard, years later. Daylight. The window has been repaired and is perfect again. Our reflection appears in it.

**NARRATOR:**
> And the money?
> More than fifty-seven million kroner was taken that morning.
> Only around six million has ever been accounted for.
> The rest, roughly fifty million kroner, has never been found.

**VISUAL:** Slow push in on the glass. In the reflection, the images from the cold open play back faintly: the burning truck, the hammer, the police car and the forest.

**NARRATOR:**
> The plan took more than a year. The robbery took minutes.
> The glass held them back twice, and the third time it broke.
> Behind it, they found more money than anyone had ever stolen in Norway.
> They also lost everything else.

**VISUAL:** The reflection fades until only the cathedral square remains, with ordinary people walking through the morning.

**NARRATOR** *(final line)*:
> Somewhere out there, fifty million kroner is still missing.
> And somewhere in Stavanger, a family still remembers the Monday he went to work… and drove toward the gunfire.

**MUSIC:** The single piano note from Chapter 5 returns and fades.

**VISUAL:** Black. Text: *In memory of Arne Sigve Klungland.*

**END.**

## Production notes

**Word count:** The narration is about 1,550 words. At a documentary pace with the written pauses and the music beats, it runs about 12 to 13 minutes. To stretch toward 14 minutes, expand Chapter 2 (the crew) or Chapter 7 (the investigation). To trim toward 10 minutes, cut the Munch *Scream* sidebar, which saves about 40 seconds.

**Recurring visual motifs (the red thread):**
1. **The glass.** It appears in the opening crack, the courtyard window, the windshield of the police car, the reflection in the epilogue, and the "VINDU" rectangle in Toska's notebook.
2. **The clock.** It starts at 03:00 and 08:00 on 5 April 2004, and it closes on 5 April 2005.
3. **The hammer sound.** It is the first sound of the film, it returns as the cell door slam, and it ends the story.

**Fact-check before publishing.** These points were confirmed in search results:
- The date (5 April 2004, about 08:00) and the place (NOKAS counting centre, Kongens gate, Stavanger city centre).
- The police HQ on Lagårdsveien was blocked by a burning truck at the garage exit, with tear gas at the entrance and spike strips on the roads.
- Entry was through the courtyard window, using a sledgehammer and a battering ram, with 113 shots fired into it.
- The robbery was the third attempt, and the glass had beaten an earlier one.
- The escape route to Sørmarka was test-driven in late winter 2004.
- About 57.4 million NOK was stolen. About 6 million has been accounted for, and about 51 million was never recovered.
- Police lieutenant Arne Sigve Klungland was killed by a shot to the head while in a police command vehicle.
- Thirteen men were convicted on 10 March 2006, with 181 years in total.
- Kjell Alrich Schumann admitted firing the fatal shots during the Gulating appeal in September 2006.
- Toska confessed on 12 April 2005 but denied shooting Klungland. He fled to Spain with his partner and their one-year-old son, and was arrested in Málaga on 5 April 2005 after surveillance and email tracing.
- Erling Havnå was known as "the sledgehammer man" (*sleggemannen*), and Metkel Betew was among those convicted.

**Verify or soften these points before publishing:**
- *The exact number of robbers at the scene.* The script says "more than a dozen" to stay safe.
- *Toska's final sentence.* Sources disagree between 18, 19 and 21 years, so the script deliberately gives no figure.
- *Havnå as a former kickboxer.* Only one secondary source said this.
- *The 600 m distance between the police HQ and NOKAS.* This is approximate; check it on a map.
- *Children or a stroller in the square.* This is a dramatic, atmospheric choice and not a documented detail.
- *The link to the theft of The Scream.* The script presents it only as a theory that was never proven, and it should stay that way.
- *Whether Klungland's children are shown as a "family".* Check before naming any relatives.
- *"Biggest robbery in Norwegian history".* This was true at the time, but it was later surpassed, so keep the words "at the time".
