### Channel rule: the five men
This case is about five friends who lost their lives, so it sits close to the rule that Spectare doesn't put victims in the spotlight. The film handles that like this:
- **The puzzle is the subject:** the road, the car, the trailer and the questions that don't add up.
- **The men appear with respect, never as spectacle.** They are named once, introduced as friends and teammates, and never shown as faces. We show their **objects** instead: five jerseys, the car, a pair of tennis shoes.
- **Bodies: stated, not shown (producer decision, 24 Nov).** The narration gives the condition of the bodies and the coroner's findings, because they are evidence: how long Ted survived, his frostbite, what was on the table, that animals had reached the others, that no wounds or weapon were found. It is said once, plainly, in the narrator's calm voice. **No body is ever shown**; the pictures show places and objects (the bed with sheets, the table, the road).
- **No implied murder:** the sources found no signs of violence. The film says so clearly and lets the viewer weigh the open questions (the sheets, the watch, Schons's lights) without suggesting a killing.
- **Disability:** said once, plainly and respectfully ("each lived with a disability of some kind"). No diagnosis labels on individuals. The case is never explained by their disabilities, and the film never makes them the joke or the "twist".
- **No accusation:** the foul-play idea is presented as a theory with what supports it and what doesn't. No one is named as a suspect.

## 1. Logline and emotional core

**Logline:** In February 1978, five friends drove to a college basketball game in Chico, California, and never came home. Their car turned up seventy miles away on a snowy mountain road, in working order. Four months later the snow melted, and the clues only got stranger.

**Emotional core:** *The road that makes no sense.* Every clue has an ordinary explanation on its own. Put together, they don't fit. The film walks the viewer up that road one clue at a time and hands them the question the investigators were left with.

**What the viewer learns:**
- How a winter search works, and why snow stops it.
- How investigators read a scene: the car, the fuel, the undercarriage, the beard growth.
- What hypothermia does to judgment.
- Why an eyewitness in pain is both important and unreliable.

### Scene 1 · Cold open: the car · 0:00–0:35 ◆
**VISUAL:**
1. Night. A turquoise-and-white 1969 Mercury Montego sits alone on a snowy mountain road, seen from behind; pines black against the sky.
2. Slow push-in.
3. Snow drifting across the headlight beams of nothing.
**NARRATION:**
[low, close] February, 1978. High in the mountains of Northern California, a car is sitting in the snow.
It isn't wrecked. It isn't out of gas. Later, when deputies start it, the engine turns over right away.
[beat] It belongs to one of five friends who drove to a basketball game the night before.
[pause] None of them were supposed to be anywhere near this mountain.
**SOUND:** Wind in pines, snow creak, a distant owl. The music holds back.

### Scene 2 · Title · 0:35–0:41
**CARD:** THE YUBA COUNTY FIVE
**VISUAL:** The case board in the dark; the lamp clicks on.

### Scene 3 · The five · 0:41–1:15
**VISUAL:**
1. A small-town street in Yuba City at dusk, 1970s.
2. Five green-and-gold jerseys laid out on a bed, neatly folded.
3. A gym, wide and empty, a ball resting on the free-throw line.
**NARRATION:**
They were from Yuba City and Marysville, two small towns in the Sacramento Valley.
Gary Mathias, twenty-five. Jack Madruga, thirty. Bill Sterling, twenty-nine. Jackie Huett, twenty-four. And Ted Weiher, thirty-two.
Each of them lived with a disability of some kind. Each of them still lived at home. And all five played on the same basketball team, the Gateway Gators.
[beat] The next morning, the Gators had a tournament game. Some of them had already laid out their uniforms.
**SOUND:** Distant gym echo, a ball bouncing once.

### Scene 4 · The night in Chico · 1:15–1:50
**VISUAL:**
1. The Mercury on a two-lane valley road at night, from behind.
2. A packed college gym, painted loose and warm; the crowd as a soft blur, no faces.
3. A small grocery store at night, lights on, a clock reading close to ten (no numbers visible: hands only).
**NARRATION:**
On Friday, February twenty-fourth, the five drove about fifty miles north to Chico State, to watch a college game. Jack Madruga drove, in his turquoise-and-white Mercury Montego.
After the game, they stopped at Behr's Market in downtown Chico, just before it closed at ten. Snacks. Sodas. Milk.
The clerk remembered them, because they delayed her closing.
[pause] It's the last time anyone is sure they saw them.
**SOUND:** Crowd swell, a buzzer, then a store door bell and quiet street.

### Scene 5 · The case board · 1:50–2:05
**VISUAL:** The case board: a topographic map of the Sierra foothills, a red pin at Chico, a red pin at Yuba City, and a third pin, far up in the mountains.
**NARRATION:**
When they didn't come home, their families called the police.
Here is what investigators found. Seven clues. And none of them fits with the others.
**SOUND:** Lamp buzz, string snap.

### Scene 6 · Clue 1: the wrong road · 2:05–2:45 ◆
**CARD:** CLUE 1 · THE WRONG ROAD
**VISUAL:**
1. The map: a red string from Chico runs south toward home, then a second string bends sharply northeast into the mountains.
2. A forest ranger, from behind, standing by the Mercury in the snow.
**NARRATION:**
The way home from Chico runs south, through the flat farm country of the valley.
Instead, the car was found about seventy miles from Chico, up a winding mountain road in the Plumas National Forest, more than four thousand feet up, at the edge of the snow.
A forest ranger had seen it there the day after the game. He didn't think much of it. People drove up that road in winter to ski.
[beat] Then he saw the missing persons bulletin.
**SOUND:** Wind; a car door creaking open.

### Scene 7 · Clue 2: the car that worked · 2:45–3:30
**CARD:** CLUE 2 · THE CAR
**VISUAL:**
1. The Montego in snow, the rear wheels in a shallow drift.
2. Push-in on the dashboard: a fuel needle at a quarter (no numbers).
3. Underneath the car, clean and undamaged.
4. On the seat, a neatly folded road map and two game programs (blank covers).
**NARRATION:**
The car was stuck, but not badly. Five young men could have pushed it out.
The keys were gone. When deputies hot-wired it, it started right away. There was a quarter of a tank of gas.
Underneath, there wasn't a scratch, not even on the low-hanging muffler, after miles of rough mountain road.
Inside were the wrappers from the store in Chico, the programs from the game, and a road map of California, neatly folded.
[pause] Whatever happened here, the car wasn't the reason they left it.
**SOUND:** The engine catching and idling; snow falling off a branch.

### Scene 8 · The storm · 3:30–3:45
**VISUAL:** A wall of snow on the mountain road; a snowcat's lights fading into white.
**NARRATION:**
That day, a heavy snowstorm hit the mountain. Searchers in snowcats nearly got lost themselves. The search was called off.
**SOUND:** Wind roar.

### Scene 9 · Clue 3: the voices in the dark · 3:45–4:55 ◆
**CARD:** CLUE 3 · THE VOICES
**VISUAL:**
1. Night, the same road. A second car, stuck in snow, its engine running; the windshield fogged, a dashboard glow inside.
2. Through the fogged glass: headlights appear behind, figures as shapes in the glare.
3. The headlights go dark.
4. Later: small flashlight beams in the trees, then gone.
**NARRATION:**
There was one more person on that road that night.
Joseph Schons, from Sacramento, had driven up to check the snow at his cabin before a family ski trip. Not far from where the Mercury would be found, he got stuck too.
While he was trying to dig out, he felt the first signs of a heart attack. He got back in the car and lay there with the engine running for heat.
Hours later, he saw headlights behind him. A group of people. He thought one of them might be a woman holding a baby.
He called out for help. [beat] The voices stopped. The lights went out.
Later he saw flashlights in the trees. He called again. [beat] Again, they went dark.
[pause] Schons was in severe pain that night, and he was never certain what he had seen. At dawn he walked about eight miles down the mountain to a lodge. On the way, he passed the Mercury. It was empty.
**SOUND:** Engine idle, a heartbeat-low pulse, muffled voices, a click, silence.

### Scene 10 · The weeks after · 4:55–5:15
**VISUAL:** The case board; flyers on the board as blank paper shapes; snow falling past a window.
**NARRATION:**
The families offered a reward. There were reported sightings. One, from a store in a small mountain town, was taken seriously, but it was never confirmed.
And the snow kept falling.
**SOUND:** Clock ticking.

### Scene 11 · Clue 4: the trailer · 5:15–6:00
**CARD:** CLUE 4 · THE TRAILER
**VISUAL:**
1. Spring. Pale green, wet snow patches, low sun through pines.
2. Motorcyclists as small distant shapes on a forest road.
3. An old Forest Service trailer in a clearing, a small shed beside it; one window broken.
**NARRATION:**
On the fourth of June, most of the snow had melted. A group of motorcyclists rode up to a Forest Service trailer at a remote camp, almost twenty miles from where the car had been found.
A window had been broken.
Inside, they found Ted Weiher, lying on a bed.
[beat] He had survived up there for weeks. Judging by his beard, perhaps around three months. Ted had been a big man, about two hundred pounds. He had lost nearly half of that. His feet were so badly frostbitten that they had begun to turn gangrenous.
He died of starvation and cold.
[pause] And that is where the case becomes hard to understand.
**SOUND:** Motorbikes fading, meltwater dripping, a door creak. Then quiet.

### Scene 12 · Clue 5: what was never used · 6:00–6:50 ◆
**CARD:** CLUE 5 · WHAT WAS NEVER USED
**VISUAL:** Slow macro push-ins, no people:
1. A cold fireplace with a box of matches and a stack of paperbacks beside it.
2. Heavy forestry coats on hooks.
3. In the shed: a stack of opened C-ration cans; behind them, a closed locker.
4. A propane tank in a second shed, its valve shut.
**NARRATION:**
The trailer had a fireplace. There were matches, and paperback books to start a fire. It was never lit.
Heavy forestry clothes hung inside. Nobody wore them.
In the shed outside, about three dozen cans of army rations had been opened and eaten. But a locker in the same shed, full of dried food, enough to feed all five of them for a year, was never opened.
And in another shed stood a fuel tank for the trailer's heater. The valve was never turned.
[pause] Warmth and food were within reach the whole time.
**SOUND:** Wind through a broken window, a tin can rolling.

### Scene 13 · Clue 6: the shoes · 6:50–7:30 ◆
**CARD:** CLUE 6 · THE SHOES
**VISUAL:**
1. On the trailer floor, a pair of white tennis shoes, alone.
2. A path of melting snow leading away from the trailer into the trees.
3. The empty road in spring light; small red evidence flags in the snowmelt at the roadside (no bodies, no bones).
4. A manzanita bush on a hillside, wide, quiet.
**GRAPHIC:** the route map; three pins drop along the road with distance counters ("11 MILES", "2 MILES FROM THE TRAILER").
**NARRATION:**
Ted Weiher's own shoes were missing.
But inside the trailer there was a pair of tennis shoes. They belonged to Gary Mathias.
[beat] Investigators believed Gary had been in the trailer too, and had walked out, perhaps in Ted's shoes.
Searchers went back up the road. About eleven miles from the car, they found Jack Madruga and Bill Sterling, on opposite sides of the road. Animals had reached them over the winter; of Bill, only bones were left.
Jackie Huett was found about two miles from the trailer, by a search party that included his father. He was identified by his shoes, his jeans, and later his dental records.
All three were believed to have died of hypothermia.
[pause] Gary Mathias was never found.
**SOUND:** A single piano note, sustained.

### Scene 13b · Clue 7: what the bodies showed · 7:30–8:20 ◆
**CARD:** CLUE 7 · WHAT THE BODIES SHOWED
**VISUAL:** Macro push-ins, no people, no bodies:
1. A bed in the trailer under a pile of white sheets, seen from the doorway in dim light (the bed only; nothing under the sheets is visible).
2. A small table: a wallet, a ring, a thin gold chain, a gold watch with a cracked crystal, a half-melted candle.
3. Ted's empty shoes are not there: the floor beside the bed, bare.
**GRAPHIC:** a coroner-style checklist built line by line in the edit: "WOUNDS: NONE · WEAPON: NONE · CAUSE: COLD AND HUNGER".
**NARRATION:**
So what did the bodies tell investigators?
No wounds. No weapon. No sign that anyone had been attacked. The deaths were put down to the cold, and in Ted's case, to hunger as well.
[beat] Robbery doesn't fit either. Ted's wallet was on the table, with cash still in it, next to a ring engraved with his name and his gold necklace.
But two things on that table don't add up.
Ted was wrapped in eight sheets, all the way over his head. With his feet in that condition, investigators believed the pain would have kept him from pulling them over himself. [beat] That suggests someone else was there with him, most likely Gary.
And next to his things lay a gold watch, its crystal missing. Ted's family said it wasn't his.
[pause] Whose watch was it?
**SOUND:** Trailer creak, a watch ticking faintly, then stopping.

### Scene 14 · "Bizarre as hell" · 8:20–8:35
**VISUAL:** The case board, wide; red string now connecting the car, the trailer and three points along the road.
**NARRATION:**
A Yuba County undersheriff summed it up for reporters: [beat] "This case is bizarre as hell."
**SOUND:** Lamp buzz.

### Scene 15 · Explanation A: lost in the snow · 8:35–9:20
**CARD:** THEORY A · LOST
**VISUAL:** The wide mountain-road plate, graded cold blue: five small silhouettes, from behind, walking up a tunnel of snow between high drifts.
**NARRATION:**
The investigators' best guess was this. A wrong turn somewhere near Oroville, maybe on the way to visit friends of Gary's in a nearby town. The car gets stuck. In the dark, someone sees tracks in the snow.
The day before, a Forest Service snowcat had driven up that road to clear the trailer's roof. Its tracks led on up the road, between drifts as tall as a person. The five may have followed them, believing shelter was close.
Cold doesn't only freeze the body. It slows thinking and judgment.
[beat] And the trailer was locked. Maybe they were afraid of being in trouble for using what wasn't theirs.
**SOUND:** Wind, footsteps in snow.

### Scene 16 · Explanation B: someone else on the mountain · 9:20–10:00
**CARD:** THEORY B · SOMEONE ELSE
**VISUAL:** The same plate graded amber: headlights in the distance, shapes beside a car, then darkness.
**NARRATION:**
But if they were lost, why leave a car that ran, and keep going deeper into the mountains, away from the lodge they had just driven past?
Who were the people Joseph Schons saw, and why did they go quiet when he called for help? Who covered Ted with the sheets, and whose watch was on the table?
[beat] The bodies showed no violence at all. No one was shot or stabbed. But no one has explained those lights.
**SOUND:** A low drone, a click, silence.

### Scene 17 · Explanation C: Gary · 10:00–10:25
**CARD:** THEORY C · GARY
**VISUAL:** The same plate graded grey dawn: one set of footprints leading away from the trailer into the trees.
**NARRATION:**
And Gary. He needed daily medication, and up there he would have gone without it. If he walked out of that trailer, where did he go?
His photo was sent to hospitals across California. Nearly fifty years later, the case is still open.
**SOUND:** Meltwater, a single bird.

### Scene 18 · The last question · 10:25–10:55
**VISUAL:**
1. The Mercury on the snowy road at dusk, from behind, the same framing as the cold open.
2. Back to the case board; the lamp clicks off.
3. In the dark, one strand of red string.
**NARRATION:**
Five friends went to a basketball game. They had a game of their own the next morning.
Somewhere on the drive home, they turned toward the mountains. [beat] And nobody knows why.
[pause] What would make five people leave a warm car and walk into the snow?
**SOUND:** Wind, then the lamp click. Two seconds of silence. Music button.
