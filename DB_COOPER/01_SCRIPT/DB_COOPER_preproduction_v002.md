# D.B. COOPER: pre-production v002

**Status:** script draft for review. Nothing has been generated: no images, voice or video. Waiting for producer feedback.
**Changes since v001 (producer note):** the emotional core moves from the people on the plane to **the mystery itself**: the clues, the theories and the suspects. The film should be exciting but educational. Spectare doesn't make films that put victims or criminals in the spotlight; the channel makes films about puzzles.
**Channel:** Spectare Animations · **Language:** English · **Audience:** adults, general audience · **Target length:** about 11:02 plus credits.

---

## 0. What carries over from THE NOKAS ROBBERY

The same rules as v001:
- **Timing:** tight timing; designed silences only.
- **Voice:** expressive narration, with a 3-voice cold-open pilot before the full voice is recorded.
- **Look:** the same gouache style.
- **Generation limits:** no lip sync, no hand work in motion, no text or logos in images, no real faces.
- **Facts:** a source ledger before the voice is recorded.
- **Packaging:** titles, thumbnails and Shorts planned up front.

**New for this film:** a *case-file structure*. After the hijacking, the film opens **six numbered clues**, then puts **five suspects** on trial against those clues, then gives **three possible endings**. Each clue and suspect has its own title card, added in the edit. This makes the film easy to follow and good for chapters. Each clue is also a ready-made Short.

**Rule for theories:** every theory is presented as a theory. For each suspect we say what fits, what doesn't, and what the FBI concluded. All five suspects are dead. We still accuse no one: the narration never says "he was Cooper".

## 1. Logline and emotional core

**Logline:** In 1971, a man who called himself Dan Cooper hijacked a plane, collected $200,000 and jumped into a stormy night over Washington. Fifty years, six clues and more than 800 suspects later, nobody can say who he was, or whether he lived to spend a dollar.

**Emotional core:** *The puzzle that won't close.* We have the tie he wore, the money he took, and the minute he jumped. Every clue points somewhere, and every theory breaks on one missing piece. The film invites viewers to play detective with us. It shows how investigators reason from evidence and where certainty runs out. It ends by handing the viewer the last question.

**What the viewer learns:** how a 1971 hijacking worked and why it changed airline security (screening and the "Cooper vane"). How the FBI tracked money by serial number. How forensic geology and diatoms date when money entered a river. How DNA and particle analysis were used on a 50-year-old tie. And why "a perfect suspect" isn't proof.

### 2.2 Style (the same look as NOKAS)
The prompt prefix is unchanged:

> *Painterly 2D gouache illustration for a serious animated documentary, graphic-novel lighting, visible brush strokes, muted palette, atmospheric haze, cinematic composition, not photorealistic; match the style of any reference images. No text, letters, numbers, signs or logos. No blood.*

Addition for this film: *1971 United States, period-accurate clothing, cars, airport and aircraft interiors; Boeing 727-100 with rear airstair; generic aircraft livery without logos.*

**New recurring set: THE CASE BOARD.** A cork board in a dim 1970s FBI office, lit by a desk lamp. Pinned paintings, red string, evidence bags with the tie, banknotes and a parachute pack. It is painted once as a master plate. Each chapter then pushes in on a different part of it; we can change what is pinned with image edits, or with layers in the edit.

**Creative freedom:** the "Dan Cooper comic" clue may use a bold, flat, mid-century European comic palette in our own style (we don't copy the real comic's art).

### 2.4 Camera language
- **Hijacking:** the same as v001 (establishing plates, slow push-ins on objects).
- **Case board:** slow lateral tracking along the board; a rack-focus effect between pinned items, done in the edit.
- **Clues:** a macro-style push-in on each object (the tie, the bills, the sand layers, the diatoms as a painted "microscope" circle).
- **Signature shot:** the open rear stairs with rain streaming past. It appears in the cold open, at 8:13 pm and in the three endings.

### 2.5 Sound palette
- **Ambience:** 727 drone, cabin hiss, intercom crackle, rain on aluminium, forest rain, river.
- **Case board:** a ticking clock, the desk lamp's buzz, paper rustle, the snap of a string being pinned.
- **Music:** a 1970s-tinged investigative motif (electric piano, upright bass, brushed snare). It rises in the clue chapters, and drops to near-silence at 8:13 pm and before the last line.
- **Chapter sting:** a soft typewriter "clack" plus a low piano note on each title card.

### 2.6 Narrator voiceover (fix for "flat and monotone")
- **Casting:** a deep, warm, curious voice, 45–60: a storyteller who loves a puzzle. More lively than NOKAS, with a hint of a smile in the clue sections and tension in the jump.
- **Process:**
  1. Generate the cold open in **3 candidate voices** on `eleven_v4`, with lower stability and performance tags.
  2. The producer picks one.
  3. Record the full voice.
- **Dialogue:** Cooper's lines use a separate quiet, flat voice through an intercom-style filter, and are never played over a visible mouth.

### Scene 1 · Cold open: 8:13 pm · 0:00–0:38 ◆
**VISUAL:** A red light blinks on a dark cockpit panel. The empty rear cabin with cigarette smoke hanging; a slow push-in down the aisle to the open rear stairs, rain streaming past. Exterior: the 727 far above a black forest.
**NARRATION:**
[low, close] Eight-thirteen in the evening. About ten thousand feet above the forests of southwest Washington.
In the cockpit, the crew feel a small jolt. The tail lifts.
[beat] At the back of the plane, the stairs are open to the rain. And the man who opened them is gone.
[pause] He has two hundred thousand dollars strapped to his body. He's wearing a business suit and loafers.
[softly] And for more than fifty years, nobody has been able to say who he was.
**SOUND:** Engine drone, wind roar, a thump in the airframe. The music cuts. 2 s of silence.

### Scene 2 · Title · 0:38–0:44
**VISUAL:** Rain on a dark cabin window. **CARD: D.B. COOPER**, with the subtitle **THE CASE THAT WON'T CLOSE**.
**SOUND:** One low electric-piano note.

### Scene 3 · Portland · 0:44–1:07
**VISUAL:** Portland airport, 1971, rain. A man in a dark raincoat at the ticket counter, seen from behind. Cash on the counter (still).
**NARRATION:**
[warm, unhurried] Portland, Oregon. The day before Thanksgiving, nineteen seventy-one.
A man in a dark suit buys a one-way ticket to Seattle. A thirty-minute flight. He pays cash.
He gives his name as Dan Cooper.
[beat] Keep that name in mind. It's our first clue. And it's wrong in more ways than one.
**SOUND:** Terminal murmur, rain on glass.

### Scene 4 · The note · 1:07–1:29 ◆
**VISUAL:** The cabin in warm cream and mustard. Cooper near the back, from behind; smoke; bourbon on the tray. A folded note handed to a flight attendant in the jump seat (still). She pockets it. He leans in: a silhouette against the window.
**NARRATION:**
He orders a bourbon and soda. Lights a cigarette. Just after takeoff, he hands a flight attendant a folded note.
She thinks it's his phone number, and pockets it without reading it.
[pause] He leans over.
**DIALOGUE:** COOPER (V.O., quiet, flat): "Miss, you'd better look at that note. I have a bomb."
**SOUND:** The music stops before his line.

### Scene 5 · The demands · 1:29–1:54
**VISUAL:** The briefcase opens a crack (still): red cylinders, wires, a battery in shadow. Cockpit from behind; radio. The plane circling at dusk over Puget Sound. Seattle on the ground: banknotes on a table, an agent at a microfilm camera with a flash of light.
**NARRATION:**
[tense] In his briefcase: red cylinders, wires, a battery.
He wants two hundred thousand dollars. Four parachutes. And a fuel truck waiting in Seattle.
The plane circles for two hours while the FBI gathers the cash. Ten thousand twenty-dollar bills.
[beat] And before they hand it over, agents photograph every single one. Every serial number.
Remember that, too.
**SOUND:** Low synth pulse, camera shutters.

### Scene 6 · The exchange · 1:54–2:26
**VISUAL:** Seattle, floodlit and raining. A heavy bag and parachute packs handed up the stairs (wide shot, no faces). Passengers walk off into the rain (a static wide shot). Cockpit: the crew listening on the intercom. Painted profile of the 727: gear down, flaps lowered, rear stairs hanging open.
**NARRATION:**
In Seattle, he trades the passengers for the money and the parachutes.
Then he gives the crew a very specific flight plan. Head for Mexico. Fly low, below ten thousand feet. Landing gear down. Flaps lowered. As slow as the plane can go.
And the rear stairs… open.
[pause] It's an odd set of instructions. Not something most people would think to ask for.
[beat] Which brings us to clue number two.
**SOUND:** Rain on the fuselage, an intercom crackle.

### Scene 7 · The jump · 2:26–3:01 ◆
**VISUAL:** Night takeoff. The last flight attendant walks to the cockpit; her point of view down the aisle: Cooper standing, tying the bag around his waist (still). The door closes. A red warning light. The aisle to the open stairs, held. Exterior: the plane over black forest, moving away. Nothing is seen falling.
**NARRATION:**
Seven-forty. They take off again. He sends the last flight attendant to the cockpit. She looks back, and sees him tying something around his waist.
Eight o'clock. A red light. The stairs are down.
[pause] Eight-thirteen. [very quiet] The jolt.
[long pause]
Two hours later, they land in Reno. The cabin is empty. He's taken the money and the briefcase. He's left behind two parachutes, eight cigarette butts, and a black clip-on tie.
**SOUND:** Takeoff roar, then the door thunk. Wind roar; the thump; **3 s of silence**; then Reno rain.

### Scene 8 · The case board · 3:01–3:20
**VISUAL:** The CASE BOARD for the first time. A dim 1970s office, desk lamp, cork wall with pinned paintings: the 727, the tie, the banknotes, the forest. Red string. A slow lateral track.
**NARRATION:**
That night, the biggest manhunt in the history of the region begins. And it starts with almost nothing.
No face. No real name. No body. No parachute.
[beat] Just a handful of clues. Let's go through them, one at a time.
**SOUND:** A clock ticking, the lamp's buzz, paper rustle. The investigative motif begins.

### Scene 9 · Clue 1: the name · 3:20–4:08
**CARD: CLUE 1 · THE NAME**
**VISUAL:** A 1971 newsroom (typewriters, smoke, no readable text). Then a gold-accented, mid-century European comic-style painting of a heroic jet pilot in flight (our own design, not the real comic's art) pinned to the board.
**NARRATION:**
Police question a man in Portland whose name really is D.B. Cooper. He has nothing to do with it. But a reporter hears the name, and the hijacker who called himself Dan becomes "D.B." Cooper. A name that was never his.
[pause] Years later, investigators notice something else. In the nineteen-fifties and sixties there was a popular comic book about a heroic test pilot. His name was Dan Cooper.
[beat] The comic was published in French. Never in English.
So did our man pick a random name? Or had he spent time in Europe or Canada… and borrowed his alias from a hero who parachutes out of planes?
**SOUND:** Typewriters, then a playful, brassy period sting for the comic.

### Scene 10 · Clue 2: what he knew · 4:08–4:57 ◆
**CARD: CLUE 2 · WHAT HE KNEW**
**VISUAL:** The 727 in profile at night, rear stairs down: a cut-away painting (no labels). From the plane window: Tacoma and an air force base far below at dusk. A ghosted painting of a cargo plane dropping supplies over jungle (period, the 1960s).
**NARRATION:**
Look at what he asked for. Four parachutes, not one. Possibly so the FBI wouldn't dare give him a faulty one, in case he took someone with him.
A speed and flap setting that suited a jump. And the rear stairs.
[pause] The Boeing seven-twenty-seven was one of the few airliners you could jump from in flight. Very few people knew that. But it had been used for covert supply drops in Southeast Asia.
On the way, he even recognised Tacoma from the air, and knew where the air force base was.
[beat] This was not a random man with a random plan. He knew planes. Maybe the military.
**SOUND:** A wind howl around the stairs; the motif with a brushed snare.

### Scene 11 · Clue 3: the night · 4:57–5:32
**CARD: CLUE 3 · THE NIGHT**
**VISUAL:** Pure indigo storm: rain lashing, the black forest below, no lights. A loafer and a suit sleeve in the wind (a still, abstracted).
**NARRATION:**
Now picture the jump. At night. In rain. Into a freezing wind. Over dense forest, with no lights below.
In a business suit. And loafers.
[pause] The FBI agent who led the case, Ralph Himmelsbach, was a pilot himself. He never believed Cooper survived. Neither did the agents who came after him.
[beat] But here's the thing. Thousands of searchers combed those woods. And not one of them found a body. Or a parachute.
**SOUND:** Storm wind, then near-silence.

### Scene 12 · Clue 4: the money · 5:32–6:02
**CARD: CLUE 4 · THE MONEY**
**VISUAL:** Blue-accented: bank tellers' counters, cash registers across the country (static vignettes), a teller holding a twenty up to the light (still). Pinned on the board: a long list painted as abstract marks (no readable numbers).
**NARRATION:**
Remember the serial numbers. The FBI sent the list to banks, casinos and businesses across the country.
If Cooper spent his money, a bill should turn up somewhere. A store. A bank. A racetrack.
[pause] Not one ever did.
[beat] For eight years, not a single bill from the ransom was found anywhere.
And then, in nineteen eighty, some of it was.
**SOUND:** A cash register ching, muffled; a single piano note on "Not one".

### Scene 13 · Clue 5: the river · 6:02–6:54 ◆
**CARD: CLUE 5 · THE RIVER**
**VISUAL:** Green-accented: a winter beach on the Columbia River, grey water. A small boy in a red jacket from behind, kneeling (still). Rotting bundles of banknotes half-buried. Then a painted cross-section of the sand showing layers, and a microscope "circle" of tiny glass-like diatoms.
**NARRATION:**
February, nineteen eighty. An eight-year-old boy is digging on a sandy bank of the Columbia River. He finds bundles of old twenty-dollar bills, rotting, still held together.
Five thousand eight hundred dollars. And the serial numbers match.
[pause] It should have solved the case. It made it stranger.
A geologist found the money lying above a layer of sand that had been dredged there in the mid-seventies. So it arrived years after the hijacking.
And decades later, independent researchers found microscopic algae on the bills. The kind that bloom in spring. Not in November.
[beat] So how did a bundle of Cooper's money end up in the river, months or years later… and only part of it?
**SOUND:** River water, wind, then the case-board clock.

### Scene 14 · Clue 6: the tie · 6:54–7:28
**CARD: CLUE 6 · THE TIE**
**VISUAL:** White-accented: the black clip-on tie in an evidence bag under a lamp. A painted microscope view: glittering particles in fabric. Then a period image of a 1960s aerospace factory floor (silhouettes, static).
**NARRATION:**
The last clue is the one he left behind. The black tie.
In two thousand one, the FBI pulled a partial DNA profile from it. Then in the twenty-tens, a team of volunteer scientists looked at the tie under an electron microscope.
They found tiny particles. Including pure titanium, and rare metals, used in only a few places at the time. Like aerospace and chemical plants.
[beat] Places where engineers wore ties to work.
**SOUND:** A soft high hum for the microscope; the motif rebuilds.

### Scene 15 · The suspects · 7:28–7:43
**VISUAL:** The case board: five paper silhouettes pinned in a row, each with red string to the clues.
**NARRATION:**
Over the years, the FBI looked at more than eight hundred suspects. Five of them come up again and again. All of them have died. None was ever proven to be Cooper.
**SOUND:** A string snapping tight, five soft taps.

### Scene 16 · Suspect 1: the copycat · 7:43–8:12
**CARD: SUSPECT · RICHARD McCOY**
**VISUAL:** Silhouette 1 (accent: steel blue) and a second 727 over desert at dawn, with the rear stairs down.
**NARRATION:**
Five months after Cooper, a man hijacked another seven-twenty-seven, took half a million dollars, and jumped out over Utah. Same method. Same stairs.
Richard McCoy. A former soldier, a skydiver. He was caught within days.
[pause] Perfect fit? Not quite. McCoy was twenty-nine. Much younger than the man the crew described. And the FBI never tied him to Flight 305.
**SOUND:** A desert wind, a lone guitar note.

### Scene 17 · Suspect 2: the insider · 8:12–8:40
**CARD: SUSPECT · KENNETH CHRISTIANSEN**
**VISUAL:** Silhouette 2 (accent: airline red) and a small suburban house in rain, plus an airline jacket on a hook (no insignia).
**NARRATION:**
Kenneth Christiansen worked for the very airline that was hijacked. He had been a paratrooper. Months after the hijacking, he bought a house, reportedly with cash. After he died, his own brother came to suspect him.
[pause] But he was shorter and slighter than the man on the plane, and the FBI did not find enough to name him.
**SOUND:** Rain on a roof.

### Scene 18 · Suspect 3: the deathbed · 8:40–8:58
**CARD: SUSPECT · DUANE WEBER**
**VISUAL:** Silhouette 3 (accent: hospital pale green) and a bedside lamp in a dark room.
**NARRATION:**
Duane Weber, on his deathbed, reportedly told his wife: "I'm Dan Cooper." She didn't know what it meant.
[beat] The FBI compared his DNA with the profile from the tie. It didn't match.
**SOUND:** A heart-monitor beep, softly, then silence.

### Scene 19 · Suspect 4: the soldier · 8:58–9:21
**CARD: SUSPECT · ROBERT RACKSTRAW**
**VISUAL:** Silhouette 4 (accent: olive drab) and a helicopter over jungle (a static painting).
**NARRATION:**
Robert Rackstraw. A Vietnam veteran with parachute training. Decades later, a team of amateur investigators built a case around him, and he became the most famous suspect of all.
[pause] The FBI had looked at him in the seventies, and ruled him out. He always denied it.
**SOUND:** A distant rotor, the motif in a minor key.

### Scene 20 · Suspect 5: the one who never existed · 9:21–9:42
**VISUAL:** Silhouette 5: an empty outline with no person inside it, a dotted line. The red strings to the other clues go slack.
**NARRATION:**
And then there's the suspect most investigators favour. A man whose name we've never heard. Who died that night in the forest. And who was never missed by anyone who connected him to a hijacking.
[beat] He fits every clue. Which is exactly the problem.
**SOUND:** The clock ticking, slowing.

### Scene 21 · Three endings · 9:42–10:21 ◆
**VISUAL:** One wide plate of the black forest from above, re-graded three times as the narration moves.
1. **Cold blue:** a canopy, rain, a torn parachute caught high in a tree, far away (no figure).
2. **Dawn gold:** a dirt road at first light; a man's back in a dark raincoat walking away into fog.
3. **Muddy green:** the river carrying a small bundle downstream under grey sky.
**NARRATION:**
So here are the endings. Pick one.
[pause] One. He didn't make it. He landed in the dark, in the cold, somewhere in that forest. And the forest kept him.
[pause] Two. He made it. He walked out of the woods, hid the money, and lived a quiet life, knowing something nobody else knew.
[pause] Three. Something in between. He made it to the river, or the money did. And the river kept the rest.
[beat] Every one of them fits some of the clues. None of them fits all.
**SOUND:** Each ending has its own sound: rain on leaves; birdsong and a distant highway; water. Then all three fall silent.

### Scene 22 · Closed · 10:21–10:41
**VISUAL:** The case board in the dim office. A hand-less shot: the evidence boxes stacked on a shelf. The lamp clicks off. Only the red string glints faintly in a street light through the blinds.
**NARRATION:**
In twenty sixteen, after forty-five years, the FBI stopped actively investigating. It's still the only unsolved hijacking of an American airliner.
But the case board never really came down. People are still out there. Checking the river. Testing the tie. Reading the old files.
**SOUND:** The lamp click. Rain on the window.

### Scene 23 · The last question · 10:41–11:02
**VISUAL:** Return to the signature shot: the open rear stairs, rain, darkness. Slow push-in until the frame is black.
**NARRATION:**
[softly] Somewhere out there is the answer. A name. A grave. Or a bundle of twenty-dollar bills, still waiting in the sand.
[long pause] He planned everything. Except one thing he couldn't control.
[beat] Whether we'd ever stop looking.
**SOUND:** Wind fades. **2 s of silence** before the last line. Rain into black.

## 4. Documented, dramatised, and theory

**Documented** (widely reported; each item to be sourced in the ledger):
- **The hijacking:**
  - 24 November 1971, Northwest Orient Flight 305, Portland to Seattle, a Boeing 727.
  - The alias "Dan Cooper", a cash ticket, the suit and tie, bourbon and cigarettes.
  - The note, the bomb line, and the briefcase with red cylinders and wires.
  - The demands ($200,000, four parachutes, fuel).
  - About two hours of circling.
  - 10,000 twenties with the serial numbers recorded on microfilm.
  - The exchange in Seattle.
  - The flight instructions (Mexico, low and slow, gear, flaps, stairs).
  - Takeoff about 7:40 pm; he was last seen tying something around his waist.
  - The stair light about 8:00 pm; the tail pitched up about 8:13 pm.
  - The landing in Reno, the empty cabin, and the items left behind (tie and clip, two parachutes, cigarette butts).
- **The case:**
  - The "D.B." name came from a Portland man who was questioned and cleared.
  - The Belgian *Dan Cooper* comic (a test pilot, published in French), which the FBI itself pointed to.
  - The aft stair, with 727 airdrops in Southeast Asia (CIA/Air America).
  - The remarks about Tacoma and McChord.
  - Himmelsbach's view that Cooper died, and later FBI statements doubting survival.
  - The large ground searches.
  - No ransom bill ever turned up in circulation.
  - 1980: Brian Ingram, Tena Bar, $5,800, serial numbers matched.
  - The geologist's dredge-layer finding.
  - The diatom finding by the Citizen Sleuths, the volunteer researcher team (spring bloom).
  - The partial DNA profile from the tie (2001), and the particle analysis of the tie (titanium, rare-earth metals).
  - More than 800 suspects; active investigation ended in 2016.
  - The Cooper vane on the 727.
- **The suspects:**
  - McCoy: the April 1972 hijacking over Utah, $500,000, arrested days later; age 29.
  - Christiansen: worked for Northwest, former paratrooper; his brother's suspicion; the physical mismatch.
  - Weber: the deathbed statement; excluded by DNA.
  - Rackstraw: a veteran, considered and ruled out by the FBI in the 1970s, revived by private investigators around 2016; he denied it.

**Dramatised:**
- the atmosphere (rain, smoke, the cabin);
- the staging of the note and the briefcase;
- the case-board set and the investigator framing;
- the newsroom;
- the abstracted "loafer in the wind" image;
- the three-endings visualisation;
- the closing lines (the narrator's own reflection).

No new dialogue is invented. Cooper's two lines and Weber's deathbed sentence are reported quotes, to be checked.

**Theory (always framed as such in the narration):**
- the comic as the source of the alias;
- the military or aviation background;
- the money being hidden or reaching the river later;
- each suspect;
- the three endings.

**⚠ To verify before the voice is recorded:**
1. The exact wording of the bomb line and of Weber's "I'm Dan Cooper".
2. "Thousands of searchers": use the sourced figure instead (hundreds of soldiers in 1972, plus later searches).
3. "The biggest manhunt in the history of the region": a superlative that needs a source, or rephrase it.
4. Whether the geologist placed the money above the 1974 dredge layer.
5. Whether the diatom finding was spring, not November, and who published it.
6. The particle findings on the tie (which elements, and the 2017 Citizen Sleuths report).
7. Christiansen's house purchase "with cash" and the height mismatch.
8. That McCoy's age was 29, and the FBI's reasons for not linking him.
9. When and how Rackstraw was ruled out.
10. Whether "no bill ever turned up" before 1980 holds; check for later claims.
11. Whether the FBI's phrasing is "the only unsolved air piracy in American aviation history".
12. Whether he left eight cigarette butts.

**Rights and dignity:**
- No real faces, and no airline logos.
- Suspects appear only as silhouettes, and the narration gives the facts against each theory as well.
- The flight crew and the boy are not given emotional storylines, and appear only as witnesses or finders.
