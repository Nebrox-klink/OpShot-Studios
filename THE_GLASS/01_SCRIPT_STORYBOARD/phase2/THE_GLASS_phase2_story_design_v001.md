# THE GLASS: Phase 2 story design (v001)

**Status:** for approval before Phase 3 (script). Nothing generated.
**Built on:** the Phase 1 dossier and `02_RESEARCH/THE_GLASS_fact_ledger_v002.csv` (F01–F65). Every beat below cites its facts. Anything not in the ledger is marked **[DRAMATISED]** or **[NEEDS SOURCE]**.
**Fixed decisions carried in:** 13:00 runtime, English, adult audience, Atlas narrator, the four existing Blender 3D sequences, faceless robber silhouettes, no gore, no invented words for Klungland.

---

## 1 · The story in one breath

**Logline (v2):**
> Grieving the friend he has just buried, a career criminal turns a robbery into a monument: eleven men, a window he swears will break "like a cracker", and twenty minutes he planned as eight. The man who pays for it is the police officer who read out the warning that morning.

**Central dramatic question:** The audience asks two questions, and the film moves from one to the other at its midpoint.
- **Acts 1 and 2A, the surface question: *will the plan work?*** The window is the test. Toska gave it 10–15 seconds (F20); it takes 113 shots and six minutes (F22, F23, F26).
- **Acts 2B and 3, the real question: *who pays for it?*** Once the glass falls the robbers are in, but late, and from that moment the plan's price starts to land on people who were never on the board: the three women in the basement, the commuters, Klungland, his family, and 32 NOKAS employees (F24, F32, F33, F57).

**Emotional core (refined from v006):**
> *Grief built the plan, and the plan made grief.*

The v006 line, "every plan has a price, and someone else pays it", still stands as the theme. Phase 1 gives it a sharper shape. The robbery began in mourning: white armbands for William Ellingsen (F15). It ended in mourning: the city's memorial for Klungland, which Toska was reading about online when he was arrested (F48, F49). The film is built between those two funerals.

**The closing image, kept from v006:** the glass. It is the thing Toska was sure he could break. What actually broke was a family.

## 2 · The glorification test

The Ellingsen angle is the strongest new material, and also the most dangerous: grief can make a robber sympathetic. Every beat must pass these six tests. Section 5 checks each risky beat against them.

| # | Test | What it means in practice |
|---|---|---|
| G1 | **Competence is shown failing, not winning.** | The plan is presented through its errors: the glass, the clock, the notes, the gate, the fire that didn't burn, the earplug. There is no "smooth heist" montage. |
| G2 | **No thrill grammar for the robbers.** | No slow motion, no hero framing, no driving heist music during their scenes. Their music is cold and sparse. Warmth and melody belong to Klungland and the staff. |
| G3 | **Grief explains; it never excuses.** | Ellingsen gets one beat. The narrator frames it as grief that made a reckless decision, and names who paid for it. A participant's own word for it, "irrational" (F07), is used. |
| G4 | **The cost is always in the frame.** | Every robbery beat from 08:00 on cuts to, or contains, the people affected: staff, commuters, police. |
| G5 | **Robbers never get the last word.** | Toska and Schumann are never given the final line of a scene in Act 3. Schumann's "mistake" is reported as his claim, next to the court's finding (F34). |
| G6 | **The ending belongs to the people who paid.** | The final two minutes are about Klungland, the staff and the missing money, not about Toska's fate. |

## 3 · Three points of view (kept from v006, with one change)

1. **David Toska, the Architect.** We are inside his plan, never inside his feelings. He is a silhouette or a back-of-head figure, with the face kept in shadow (a living convicted person; see Phase 4).
2. **Arne Sigve Klungland, the Officer.** Seen only from outside, with restraint. **Change:** he enters the film much earlier, in Act 2A, as the police side prepares. Phase 1 shows he was the one who briefed the patrols that morning (F12). This creates the film's main dramatic irony (section 4): the audience knows both rooms that morning; neither room knows the other.
3. **The Glass.** The silent witness. It appears four times, once in each act. The last time, it is intact.

## 4 · Dramatic irony and surprise plan

**Irony** means the audience knows something a character doesn't. **Surprise** means we withhold it and reveal it later. Each item below is one or the other, by design.

| # | What the audience knows | Who doesn't | Planted | Paid off | Type |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| I1 | The window is special armoured glass | Toska ("kjeks", 10–15 s) | Cold open (it doesn't break) | 3D 2 "113" | Irony |
| I2 | The police have a 13-page warning and extra revolvers | The robbers | Beat 10 | Beats 12, 17 | Irony |
| I3 | A robbery crew is putting on white armbands across town | Klungland, reading the warning | Beat 11 | Beat 12 (cross-cut) | Irony |
| I4 | Klungland drives a bus for pensioners | Nobody; it is simply true | Beat 10 (portrait) | Beat 19: "a bus behind him", **no comment** | Irony, left unspoken |
| I5 | The burning is meant to destroy DNA | The robbers believe it will work | Beat 8 | Beat 20: it doesn't | Irony |
| I6 | Three women have already taken 60 million into the lift | The robbers inside | Beat 15 | Beat 23 (60 saved vs 57.4 taken) | Irony |
| I7 | The barred vault gate was **unlocked** | Everyone, including the audience until late | Beat 16 (we *see* the gate and the ram fail) | Beat 23 (police photo reveal) | **Surprise** |
| I8 | The police have been reading his email | Toska | Beat 24 | Beat 25 | Irony |
| I9 | The date: 5 April, one year on | Toska, reading about the memorial | Beat 25 | Beat 25 | Irony |

**Why I7 (the unlocked gate) is a late surprise, not running irony:** if the audience knew during the robbery, the beat would play as "so close": a heist-film frustration that invites the viewer to want the robbers to succeed (fails G1 and G2). Revealed after the killing, it plays as the opposite: the man who "thought of everything" never tried the handle, and a man died during the minutes it cost. *Verify F28 against the judgment if possible: it is High confidence (NRK, police photo) but single-outlet.*
