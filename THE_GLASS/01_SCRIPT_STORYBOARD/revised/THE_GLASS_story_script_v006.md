# THE GLASS: story script v006 (draft for approval)

**Status:** draft for review. No images, voice or video will be generated until this is approved.
**Event:** the NOKAS robbery, Stavanger, Norway, Monday 5 April 2004
**Target length:** 13:00 | **Narration:** English | **Audience:** adults, general public
**Narrator:** ElevenLabs "Verdict". A deep, calm male voice, 40–60, warm but serious, like a storyteller by a fire.

---

## 1 · Logline and emotional core

**Logline:** A chess-playing career criminal spends months engineering the perfect eight-minute robbery of Norway's best-guarded cash depot. One window he misjudged and one police officer driving toward the gunfire turn his masterpiece into the crime a whole country never forgives.

**Emotional core:** *Every plan has a price, and someone else pays it.* Toska thinks several moves ahead, like a chess player. The story lets the viewer admire the mechanism for a while, then slowly shows the cost he never put on the board: a husband and father of two who went to work on a Monday. The window is the story's central image. It is the thing he was sure he could break. In the end, the thing that shatters is a family, and Toska's own idea of himself as the man who had thought of everything.

**Three points of view:**
1. **David Toska, the Architect.** Intellect, control, pride. The viewer is inside his plan.
2. **Arne Sigve Klungland, the Officer.** Duty and ordinary decency. Seen only from the outside, with restraint and no invented inner monologue.
3. **The Glass.** The non-human "character" at the centre: the counting-room window, which holds and holds until it gives.

**Dramatic irony used throughout:**
- The viewer learns early that the window is armoured. Toska believes it is ordinary glass.
- The viewer is told that burning the mattresses will hand the police their DNA, before the gang lights them.
- The viewer meets Klungland at the start of his day. The gang doesn't know he exists.

## 2 · Character sheet and visual style guide

### Style
- **Look:** cinematic 2D with painterly backgrounds (gouache texture, soft edges, visible brushwork in the skies and in wet asphalt), clean character line art (2 px dark-umber line, not black), and limited but expressive animation: held poses, breathing, blinks, cloth and smoke on loops. Animatic-plus quality, like modern animated documentaries.
- **Camera:** establishing wides on parallax layers, slow push-ins for tension, held close-ups on hands and eyes at decision points, top-down "chessboard" maps for the plan, and handheld shake only in the gunfire scenes.
- **Rule for the robbers:** they are never glamorised. They appear as dark, faceless silhouettes with masks or helmets. No hero shots, no slow-motion gun poses. Only Toska has a visible face, and only before and after the robbery.
- **Rule for violence:** no blood, no wounds, no bodies. Gunfire is shown through muzzle light on walls, cracks in glass and sound. Klungland's death is told through absence: an empty frame, silence.

### Characters

**David Toska** (real person, ringleader). Born 1975 in Bergen, so 28–29 in 2004.
- Design: slim, average height, short dark hair, clean-shaven. Alert, watchful eyes and a still face. He rarely moves when he thinks.
- Clothing: dark zip jacket, plain sweaters, jeans. In Spain, a light shirt.
- Palette: ink blue and charcoal, with one ivory accent (the chess motif).
- Signature visual: his hands moving pieces on a chessboard that becomes a map of Stavanger.
- Likeness: stylised, based on press photos. No caricature.

**Arne Sigve Klungland** (real person, police lieutenant; victim).
- Design: Norwegian police uniform of 2004 (dark navy, chequered band, reflective vest in the vehicle). Calm, steady face.
- **Age and likeness must be confirmed from an approved public photo, and ideally with the family's knowledge before publication.**
- Palette: navy and a single warm skin tone. He is the only warm figure in the cold scenes.
- Treatment: he never speaks invented lines. We hear only his radio and his engine.

**Kjell Alrich Schumann** (real person, the shooter).
- Design: a robber silhouette, faceless in helmet and mask, heavier build. He is never shown as a personality, only as a force. He is named by the narrator only.

**The crew** (real; 13 were convicted).
- Design: interchangeable black-clad silhouettes, with numbers rather than faces in the plan scenes, shown as chess pawns on the map.

**NOKAS staff** (real people, unnamed).
- Design: ordinary office workers in 2004 cardigans, shirts and fleece. Faces softly drawn, never shown in close-up terror. Shown fleeing, not cowering.

**The Glass** (the counting-room window on the courtyard).
- Design: thick, faintly green laminated glass in a dark steel frame. Each act shows it in a new state: perfect, watched, hammered, cracked, opened, and finally replaced.

### Period accuracy (2004)
- Vehicles: Norwegian police Volvo V70 and a command vehicle (van type: verify), a truck (verify type), and a city bus (Kolumbus livery of 2004: verify).
- Technology: Nokia-era mobile phones, analogue police radio, a CRT monitor and PC tower in a 2005 Spanish internet café.
- Places: Domkirkeplassen and Stavanger Cathedral (12th century), Kongsgata, and wooden houses in Old Stavanger. Verify the NOKAS building's façade and courtyard against archive photos.

## 3 · Full script, scene by scene

### Scene 1 · Cold Open: "08:03" (0:00–0:45)

**VISUAL:** Black. A single crack appears, white and branching. Pull back: we're inside a quiet counting room, looking at a thick green window from inside. Grey morning light. Outside, a black silhouette swings a sledgehammer. *Thud.* The glass shivers and holds. A second silhouette raises a rifle. The camera slowly pushes in on the glass as the first bullet star blooms.

**NARRATION:**
> Eight o'clock on a Monday morning.
> Stavanger. One of the safest cities in the world.
> *(beat)*
> A man in black is standing in a courtyard, firing a rifle into a window.
> And the window… is not breaking.

**SOUND:** Total silence, then a deep, muffled *thud* (the hammer heard from inside). A low sub-bass drone. One gunshot, muffled, with a glassy ring. Hard cut to silence.

---

### Scene 2 · Title (0:45–0:55)

**VISUAL:** The cracks in the glass rearrange into the title: **THE GLASS**. Beneath, in small type: *Stavanger, Norway. 5 April 2004.*

**NARRATION:**
> To understand how a man ends up there, we have to go back. To a chessboard.

**SOUND:** One low piano note, held.

---

### Scene 3 · The City of Money (0:55–1:50)

**VISUAL:** Painterly aerial of Stavanger at golden hour: white wooden houses, the harbour, platform supply ships. A slow pan to the cathedral, and next to it an ordinary old office block. Inside: hands counting notes, the gentle clatter of counting machines, sealed bags on a trolley. The camera finds the window from inside: thick and green-tinted, with a dark courtyard beyond.

**NARRATION:**
> Stavanger is a small city that struck it rich. Since the seventies, oil from the North Sea has poured money into this coastline.
> And money, real money, still needs a room. A room where it is counted, sorted and sealed.
> In 2004, that room was here. NOKAS, Norway's cash-handling service, right in the heart of the city, beside a nine-hundred-year-old cathedral.
> It didn't look like a fortress. It didn't need to. Its real defence was the glass. Thick, layered, built to take punishment.
> Remember this window.

**SOUND:** Gulls, distant church bells, a soft rhythmic counting-machine whirr that becomes the music's pulse.

---

### Scene 4 · The Chess Player (1:50–3:00)

**VISUAL:** An Oslo flat at night, ink-blue light. A young man sits at a chessboard, alone, perfectly still. Close-up on his eyes, then his hand moving a knight. Montage on the chessboard: pieces fall and the board briefly becomes a newspaper headline, a rammed shop window and a police search. Toska sits through it all without moving.

**NARRATION:**
> David Toska grew up in Oslo. As a boy he was good at chess, a game you win by seeing several moves ahead.
> He took that habit somewhere else.
> Before he was twenty, he was arrested over a robbery where a car was driven straight through a shop window.
> In 1996, police searched his home and found a submachine gun, explosives and a complete set of robbery tools.
> Detectives came to believe he was the quiet planner behind several big break-ins. Not the man with the gun. The man with the plan.

**DIALOGUE (dramatised, inner voice):**
> TOSKA *(quietly, to himself)*: Everyone plans the robbery. Nobody plans the police.

**SOUND:** The tick of a chess clock. Low, minimal synth. The click of each piece placed lands on the beat.

---

### Scene 5 · A Blind Spot (3:00–3:50)

**VISUAL:** Late 2003. A smoky kitchen table. A contact's hand (faceless) slides a sketch of a building across. Toska leans in. The chessboard dissolves into a top-down map of central Stavanger: the NOKAS building as the king, and the police station a short drive away as a rook.

**NARRATION:**
> In late 2003, someone told him about NOKAS. About a building with a blind spot.
> Toska saw the board instantly. The money could be beaten. Walls can be beaten. Guards can be beaten.
> The real problem was the police. They would always come.
> *(beat)*
> Unless they couldn't get out.
> So the plan had two halves. One team would break into the counting room. The other would lock the police inside their own station.

**DIALOGUE (dramatised):**
> CONTACT *(off-screen)*: It's an old building. Nobody thinks anyone would dare.
> TOSKA: Then that's where we go.

**SOUND:** The kitchen hum. A low string note rises when the police "rook" appears on the map.

---

### Scene 6 · The Crew (3:50–4:25)

**VISUAL:** On the map, pawns appear one by one: faceless black figures. Names appear as typed labels for only two of them, *Erling Havnå* and *Kjell Alrich Schumann*. Schumann's piece is drawn slightly heavier.

**NARRATION:**
> He built a crew. Old prison contacts, school friends, new faces. More than a dozen men.
> Among them, Kjell Alrich Schumann.
> Keep that name in mind. It will matter more than any other.

**SOUND:** Each pawn lands with a heavy wooden *clack*. Then silence on Schumann's name.

---

### Scene 7 · February ★ 3D 1, "Through the Glass" (4:25–5:20)

**VISUAL:** Night, February. Frost on the cobbles. Toska crouches below the counting-room window, breath steaming. Through the green glass: staff wheeling trolleys of cash away, *out* of reach. His eyes follow the money. He waits. He looks at his watch. He stands and walks away into the dark.

**NARRATION:**
> Here is the part most people never hear.
> Two months before the robbery, Toska was already here. In the courtyard. Below the window. In the freezing dark.
> Through the glass, he watched the money being wheeled away.
> The timing was wrong. So he did the hardest thing a planner can do.
> He went home.

**SOUND:** Wind, a distant car, his breathing. Through the glass, the muffled squeak of trolley wheels. No music until he stands up, then one cold, sustained note.

*(Source note: this episode rests on secondary sources S04/S05. Toska later told the court it was never an attempt.)*

### Scene 8 · Rehearsal (5:20–6:05)

**VISUAL:** Late winter. A montage of a car on wet roads, then men on foot through underpasses and along cycle paths, out to dark forest at Sørmarka. A stopwatch overlay. Back on the chessboard map, Toska taps the window square. **Dramatic irony:** a cross-section graphic shows the viewer the window's layered, armoured construction, while Toska writes "glass: ordinary" in his notebook.

**NARRATION:**
> All through the late winter, they rehearsed the escape. From the city centre to the woods at Sørmarka. Over and over. Where a car would be noticed, they walked.
> By April, they knew those streets better than the police did.
> Toska's plan gave them eight minutes. Eight minutes in, eight minutes out.
> And it rested on one assumption. That the old window in the courtyard was ordinary glass.
> *(beat)*
> It was not.

**SOUND:** The stopwatch tick merges with footsteps. Rain. A sharp, glassy "ting" on "It was not."

---

### Scene 9 · 03:30 (6:05–6:45)

**VISUAL:** 5 April 2004, before dawn. Sodium-amber streetlight outside a house near Stavanger. A van; men loading heavy bags (vests, helmets, spike strips; no lingering on weapons). Cut across town: an ordinary police locker room, a lieutenant buttoning his uniform, seen from behind, unhurried. He picks up his cap. We do not see his face yet.

**NARRATION:**
> Monday, the fifth of April. Half past three in the morning.
> Bulletproof vests. Helmets. Spike strips. Smoke grenades.
> It was Easter week. The police were short-staffed. And a warning had already named NOKAS as a possible target.
> *(beat)*
> Across the city, another man was getting ready for work.

**SOUND:** Van doors, zips, the clink of metal. Then a contrast: a quiet locker door, a radio murmuring weather, a tap dripping.

*(Dramatised: the locker-room moment. We do not know Klungland's morning. It's shown as a generic, wordless image of a shift starting.)*

---

### Scene 10 · Lock the Door (6:45–7:25)

**VISUAL:** 08:00. A truck rolls across the police garage exit and bursts into flames. Smoke canisters bounce at the main entrance. Spike strips unroll across the roads. Inside: officers at the garage door, blocked by fire. **Dramatic irony:** a slow push-in on the truck's cargo (mattresses and towels from the hideout) as flames climb them.

**NARRATION:**
> Eight o'clock. The first move.
> A truck is set alight across the police garage exit. Tear gas at the front door. Spikes across the roads.
> The police of Stavanger are locked inside their own building.
> The truck was packed with mattresses and towels from the gang's hideout. Burning them would destroy their DNA.
> *(beat)*
> It did the opposite. Those mattresses would deliver the gang to the police.

**SOUND:** A diesel engine, a whoomp of ignition, alarms, coughing. The music becomes a fast, low ostinato.

---

### Scene 11 · 113 ★ 3D 2, "113" (7:25–8:05)

**VISUAL:** The courtyard. A sledgehammer: the glass holds. A battering ram: the glass holds. Toska raises a rifle and fires. Then he signals. A heavier silhouette steps up (Schumann) and fires magazine after magazine. The cracks spread into branching layers. Finally the glass sags inward, a hole tears open and a gloved hand reaches through.

**NARRATION:**
> The second move. The window.
> A sledgehammer. A battering ram. The glass held.
> Toska fired first. Then he called for Schumann.
> *(silence over the gunfire, about 6 s)*
> One hundred and thirteen shots.
> *(beat)*
> And the glass… gives.

**SOUND:** Impacts heard from inside the room: muffled, glassy, each shot a little louder as the glass weakens. The music drops out completely. The final tearing of the glass is the loudest sound in the film, then a sudden quiet.

---

### Scene 12 · Monday Morning (8:05–8:45)

**VISUAL:** Inside the counting room. Staff run for the far doors. One woman grabs a colleague's sleeve, and they get out. Black figures climb through the hole. Bags of notes are thrown from the cages into the courtyard. A wall clock ticks past 08:08.

**NARRATION:**
> Inside were people who had come to work on a Monday morning. Several of them got out.
> The robbers emptied the room. Bag after bag. Fifty-seven million kroner, at the time the biggest robbery in Norwegian history.
> But the eight minutes were already gone.
> Outside, on the cathedral square, the first police officers were arriving.

**SOUND:** Running feet, a door slamming, shouts muffled through walls. The clock tick becomes the music's pulse again. Distant sirens.

---

### Scene 13 · Kongsgata (8:45–9:20)

**VISUAL:** Not every officer was trapped. A police command vehicle drives along Kongsgata, near the Maritim hotel. For the first time we see the lieutenant's face: calm and focused. Through the windscreen ahead, smoke rises over the rooftops.

**NARRATION:**
> Not every officer in Stavanger was trapped behind that burning truck.
> Police lieutenant Arne Sigve Klungland was already out. A husband. A father of two.
> He was in a command vehicle on Kongsgata.
> And like the officers on the square… he was driving toward the gunfire.

**SOUND:** The engine, wipers, police radio chatter (indistinct, generic). Music: a single sustained, warm cello note. It is the only warm sound in this act.

*(Dramatised: the radio chatter and his expression. Documented: his rank, the vehicle, his location and the direction of travel.)*

---

### Scene 14 · 08:17 ★ 3D 3, "The Windshield" (9:20–9:55)

**VISUAL:** The command vehicle from outside, wide and still. A bus blocks the road behind. The vehicle tries to reverse. A muzzle flash is reflected only in a shop window across the street. Cut to: the windscreen, cracking. Fade to white. No body, no wound, no blood.

**NARRATION:**
> Seventeen minutes past eight.
> *(no narration for the rest of the scene)*

**SOUND:** The engine reversing, the bus horn, then gunshots heard only as a distant, flat sound. Then nothing: the music cuts. About 6 seconds of complete silence over white.

---

### Scene 15 · Remember His Name (9:55–10:20)

**VISUAL:** Soft warm grey. Candles at the cathedral square. A simple drawn portrait of Klungland in uniform, based on an approved photo, held for a long time. Slow, slow push-in.

**NARRATION:**
> He tried to back away, but a bus blocked the road. One of the bullets struck him.
> Arne Sigve Klungland died in the middle of his own city, on a Monday morning.
> He grew up in Flekkefjord. At home in Riska, he was active in his church.
> Remember his name.

**SOUND:** A quiet hymn-like piano. Candle flicker. No sound design beyond that.

---

### Scene 16 · Into the Trees (10:20–10:50)

**VISUAL:** 08:19. Cars leave the square. The rehearsed route: underpasses, cycle paths, then the dark forest of Sørmarka. Vehicles are swapped. Smoke rises as evidence burns. The black silhouettes walk into the trees and dissolve. Close-up on Toska's face in a car mirror. For the first time, the stillness is gone.

**NARRATION:**
> At nineteen minutes past eight, they fled. Along the route they had practised all winter, into the woods.
> Within minutes, the men in black were gone.
> Toska had planned to beat a window, a police station, a city.
> He had not planned to kill a police officer.
> And from that moment, Norway would not rest until every one of them was found.

**SOUND:** Car engines fade into forest: wind in pine, crows. The music returns, now in a minor key.

---

### Scene 17 · The Hunt (10:50–11:35)

**VISUAL:** An investigation room in fluorescent green: a wall of photos and red string. One by one, faces get a red X. A lab bench holds a scorched mattress sample. Then Toska in Sandnes, curtains drawn, then on a road south, then in Spanish sunlight. Close-up on a cheap laptop: the same email login, typed again and again.

**NARRATION:**
> It became one of the biggest investigations Norway had ever seen. The mattresses held their DNA. Arrests followed, across the country.
> One photo on the board had no X. The man the newspapers called the mastermind.
> Toska hid for days, then drove to Spain. His partner and their young son followed.
> He was careful about almost everything.
> *(beat)*
> But he kept using the same email address.

**SOUND:** A phone ringing, printer noise, the squeak of a marker on the board. In Spain: cicadas, scooters, a keyboard clicking.

---

### Scene 18 · One Year to the Day ★ 3D 4 (11:35–12:05)

**VISUAL:** A small internet café in Málaga: CRT monitors and plastic chairs. Toska at a terminal, reading. The page loads slowly. Behind him, the door opens. Spanish police officers walk in quietly. He doesn't resist. The screen goes dark.

**NARRATION:**
> Investigators traced his logins to the south of Spain. The provider slowed his connection to keep him online.
> On the fifth of April 2005, Spanish police walked into an internet café in Málaga.
> *(beat)*
> One year to the day.

**SOUND:** Street noise and a fan humming. A slow modem-era loading sound. The door bell. Then the CRT's power-down *whine* to silence.

*(Some sources give 6 April 2005. The Aftenbladet and Wikipedia sources give 5 April. Resolve before locking the date graphic.)*

---

### Scene 19 · The Verdict (12:05–12:35)

**VISUAL:** A courtroom in Stavanger, drawn in muted slate. Thirteen silhouettes in a row. The judge's hands. Numbers appear like chalk on a slate: *13 convicted · 181 years in total · Toska: 19 years.* Then the appeal court in September 2006: a single silhouette stands up.

**NARRATION:**
> Toska admitted planning the robbery. He denied firing the shot that killed Arne Sigve Klungland.
> In March 2006, thirteen men were convicted. Together, a hundred and eighty-one years. Toska received nineteen.
> Six months later, in the appeal court, one man finally said it out loud.
> Kjell Alrich Schumann fired the shots that killed Arne Sigve Klungland.

**SOUND:** Courtroom ambience: paper, a cough, a gavel-free hush (Norwegian courts don't use gavels). Music: low strings, restrained.

---

### Scene 20 · The Glass (12:35–13:00)

**VISUAL:** The present day. The same courtyard window, now new, clear and unbroken, in warm morning light. Reflected in it: the cathedral, people walking past. Slow push-in until the glass fills the frame. On it, faintly, a reflection of the candle-lit portrait from Scene 15. Then black.

**NARRATION:**
> More than fifty-one million kroner has never been found.
> Toska served thirteen years and walked out on parole in 2018.
> He had thought several moves ahead. He had planned for the glass, the police, the city.
> *(beat)*
> But the only thing in this story that could never be replaced… was never on his board.

**SOUND:** The same single low piano note as the title, then one more note above it, resolving. Morning birds. Silence over black for 2 s.
