## HOW TO READ THIS SCRIPT

Every part of the script is marked with what kind of animation it is:

> 🟦 **2D ANIMATION** is the narrated story, in the main 2D style. It is used for most of the film.

> 🟥 **3D CINEMATIC** is one of four 30-second 3D sequences, each at a turning point in the story.

Each marker gives the timecode and the length. A 🟥 3D block always ends with a **⬛ END 3D, BACK TO 2D** line, so it is always clear where 3D starts and stops.

## Format notes

- **NARRATOR**: one voice from start to finish. Calm, low and controlled. They never shout, and the tension comes from restraint.
- **VISUAL** lines are directions for the animation team. **SFX / MUSIC** are cues for the sound designer.
- Pacing assumes about 140 words per minute of narration plus pauses. Timestamps are targets.
- **Red thread: the glass.** One bulletproof window runs through the whole story. In February, Toska watched the money through it and called the robbery off. In April, he assumed it was ordinary glass, and it took 113 shots to break. Everything after that point also breaks: the plan, the getaway and the gang.
- **Second motif: the clock.** 5 April 2004 opens the story, and 5 April 2005 closes it.
- **Tone:** a police officer died in this robbery. Make it thrilling, but do not glorify it. Show no gore, and keep the robbers as silhouettes or masked figures. Arne Sigve Klungland is the one person shown with warmth.

### Rules for the 🟥 3D sequences
- **In and out through glass.** Each 3D sequence begins when the 2D picture cracks and shatters into 3D space. It ends when the 3D image is caught in a pane of glass, which flattens back into 2D.
- **Length:** exactly 30 seconds each. They are short, intense peaks, so every shot has to count.
- **Narration:** at most 1–2 short lines in each 3D sequence. The pictures carry it.
- **Look:** grounded, cinematic lighting, but stylised rather than photoreal. Use a slightly desaturated palette, volumetric light and shallow depth of field. The cameras should feel physical.
- **Characters** match the 2D designs in silhouette, so viewers recognise them across the switch. Robbers stay masked and faceless.
- **Sound** becomes fully spatial: the score drops away and room tone, wind and distance take over.

## CHAPTER 1 — THE CITY OF MONEY

> 🟦 **2D ANIMATION** · 0:45 – 2:00 · *(1:15)*

**VISUAL:** Aerial animation of Stavanger at dawn: white wooden houses, the harbour, offshore supply ships. Oil platforms glow on the horizon like distant cities.

**NARRATOR:**
> Stavanger, on Norway's west coast. A small city that struck it rich.
> Since the 1970s, oil from the North Sea has made it one of the wealthiest places on the planet.
> Where there is money, there is cash. And cash has to be counted somewhere.

**VISUAL:** Push in through the old town to the cathedral square. A plain, solid building on Kongens gate. A sign reads **NOKAS – Norsk Kontantservice**.

**NARRATOR:**
> That somewhere was NOKAS, Norway's cash-handling service.
> Behind these walls, millions of kroner moved every day. They were counted, sorted, sealed and sent out to banks across the region.
> The building sat in the middle of town, by the cathedral, the shops and a busy pedestrian street.
> Hiding in plain sight was its security. Nobody would dare rob a vault in the middle of the city.

**VISUAL:** Inside, we follow a cash cart past a thick window that looks out on a back courtyard. Hold on the glass. It is greenish and layered, and it reflects the room.

**NARRATOR:**
> The building's real defence wasn't a gun or a guard.
> It was glass. Armoured, layered, and built to take punishment.
> Remember this window.

## Production notes

**Runtime:** about 13:00, with 11:00 of 🟦 2D and 2:00 of 🟥 3D (4 × 30 seconds). The narration is about 1,150 words. The rest of the time goes to pauses, music beats and the 3D sequences, which are almost silent.

**3D production priorities**, from the most to the least demanding:
1. **🎬 2 "113"** has slow-motion glass simulation, muzzle flashes and the climax of the film. Budget it first.
2. **🎬 4 "One Year to the Day"** needs a simple Málaga street, an early-2000s internet-café interior (PC towers, CRT monitors) and 3–4 figures.
3. **🎬 1 "Through the Glass"** needs one courtyard set, frost, a lit window and breath condensation. The courtyard and window are **reused in 🎬 2**, relit for daylight.
4. **🎬 3 "The Windshield"** needs one vehicle interior and one street. It is the simplest in polygons but the hardest in tone. Test it with an audience before final render.

**Shared 3D assets:** the NOKAS courtyard and window (🎬 1 and 2), the glass-crack shader (all four), the masked robber rig (🎬 2 and 3), the Toska silhouette rig (🎬 1 and 4), the police vehicle (🎬 3) and the cathedral spire (🎬 3).

**Recurring motifs (the red thread):**
1. **The glass.** It appears in the opening crack, the courtyard window, the windshield, the car window in Málaga and the reflection in the epilogue. Glass is also the doorway every time the film passes between 🟦 2D and 🟥 3D.
2. **The clock.** It starts at 03:00 and 08:00 on 5 April 2004, and it closes on 5 April 2005.
3. **The hammer sound.** It is the first sound of the film, it hits the window in 🎬 2, and it returns as the last sound of 🎬 4.

**Fact-check before publishing.** These points were confirmed in search results:
- The date (5 April 2004, about 08:00) and the place (NOKAS counting centre, Kongens gate, Stavanger city centre).
- The police HQ on Lagårdsveien was blocked by a burning truck at the garage exit, with tear gas at the entrance and spike strips on the roads.
- Entry was through the courtyard window, using a sledgehammer and a battering ram, with 113 shots fired into it.
- In February 2004 Toska waited in the NOKAS courtyard, saw through the window that money was being moved out of the basement, and called it off. In court he denied it was an attempt. Some sources call 5 April the third attempt. Toska believed the window was ordinary glass.
- The escape route to Sørmarka was test-driven in late winter 2004.
- About 57.4 million NOK was stolen. About 6 million has been accounted for, and about 51 million was never recovered.
- Police lieutenant Arne Sigve Klungland was killed by a shot to the head while in a police command vehicle.
- Thirteen men were convicted on 10 March 2006, with 181 years in total.
- Kjell Alrich Schumann admitted firing the fatal shots during the Gulating appeal in September 2006.
- Toska confessed on 12 April 2005 but denied shooting Klungland. He fled to Spain with his partner and their one-year-old son, and was arrested in Málaga on 5 April 2005 after surveillance and email tracing.
- Erling Havnå was known as "the sledgehammer man" (*sleggemannen*), and Metkel Betew was among those convicted.

**Dramatised details in the 3D sequences.** These are staging choices, not documented facts:
