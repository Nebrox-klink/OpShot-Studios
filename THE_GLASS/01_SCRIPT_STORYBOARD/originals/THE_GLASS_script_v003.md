# THE GLASS — The NOKAS Robbery
### Animated documentary script · Runtime: ~13:00 · One narrator · 2D animation with four 30-second 3D cinematic sequences

---

## HOW TO READ THIS SCRIPT

Every part of the script is marked with what kind of animation it is:

> 🟦 **2D ANIMATION** is the narrated story, in the main 2D style. It is used for most of the film.

> 🟥 **3D CINEMATIC** is one of four 30-second 3D sequences, each at a turning point in the story.

Each marker gives the timecode and the length. A 🟥 3D block always ends with a **⬛ END 3D, BACK TO 2D** line, so it is always clear where 3D starts and stops.

## Format notes

- **NARRATOR**: one voice from start to finish. Calm, low and controlled. They never shout, and the tension comes from restraint.
- **VISUAL** lines are directions for the animation team. **SFX / MUSIC** are cues for the sound designer.
- Pacing assumes about 140 words per minute of narration plus pauses. Timestamps are targets.
- **Red thread: the glass.** One bulletproof window runs through the whole story. It beat the gang twice, and the third time it broke. Everything after that point also breaks: the plan, the getaway and the gang.
- **Second motif: the clock.** 5 April 2004 opens the story, and 5 April 2005 closes it.
- **Tone:** a police officer died in this robbery. Make it thrilling, but do not glorify it. Show no gore, and keep the robbers as silhouettes or masked figures. Arne Sigve Klungland is the one person shown with warmth.

### Rules for the 🟥 3D sequences
- **In and out through glass.** Each 3D sequence begins when the 2D picture cracks and shatters into 3D space. It ends when the 3D image is caught in a pane of glass, which flattens back into 2D.
- **Length:** exactly 30 seconds each. They are short, intense peaks, so every shot has to count.
- **Narration:** at most 1–2 short lines in each 3D sequence. The pictures carry it.
- **Look:** grounded, cinematic lighting, but stylised rather than photoreal. Use a slightly desaturated palette, volumetric light and shallow depth of field. The cameras should feel physical.
- **Characters** match the 2D designs in silhouette, so viewers recognise them across the switch. Robbers stay masked and faceless.
- **Sound** becomes fully spatial: the score drops away and room tone, wind and distance take over.

## COLD OPEN

> 🟦 **2D ANIMATION** · 0:00 – 0:45 · *(0:45)*

**VISUAL:** Pure black. Then one hairline crack appears across the screen, as if the viewer is looking through glass.

**SFX:** A single sledgehammer strike on armoured glass. Deep and dull, with no echo. Then total silence.

**NARRATOR** *(00:01)*:
> At eight o'clock on a Monday morning, in one of the safest cities in the world…
> the police could not get out of their own police station.

**SFX:** A truck engine roars, then the *whoomp* of fire catching.

**VISUAL:** Quick cut to a stylised police garage ramp. A white truck sits across the exit and bursts into flames. Orange light floods the frame.

**NARRATOR:**
> Their exit was burning. Spikes were spread across the roads. Smoke drifted past the front door.
> And a short drive away, in the shadow of a 900-year-old cathedral, men in black were firing into a window.

**SFX:** Automatic fire, muffled as if heard from inside a building. It keeps going and does not stop.

**NARRATOR:**
> They fired one hundred and thirteen bullets into one window.
> This is the story of that window. Of the men who spent a year trying to break it.
> And of what it cost when they finally did.

**VISUAL:** The crack spreads across the whole screen and the glass SHATTERS toward the camera. Each shard reflects a different face that we will meet later: a ringleader, a sledgehammer, a policeman, a seafront in Spain.

**MUSIC:** A hard hit, then the title.

### TITLE CARD: **THE GLASS — The NOKAS Robbery · Stavanger, 5 April 2004**

## CHAPTER 1 — THE CITY OF MONEY

> 🟦 **2D ANIMATION** · 0:45 – 2:00 · *(1:15)*

**VISUAL:** Aerial animation of Stavanger at dawn: white wooden houses, the harbour, offshore supply ships. Oil platforms glow on the horizon like distant cities.

**NARRATOR:**
> Stavanger, on Norway's west coast. A small city that struck it rich.
> Since the 1970s, oil from the North Sea has made it one of the wealthiest places on the planet.
> Where there is money, there is cash. And cash has to be counted somewhere.

**VISUAL:** Push in through the old town to the cathedral square. A plain, solid building on Kongens gate. A sign reads **NOKAS – Norsk Kontantservice**.

**NARRATOR:**
> That somewhere was NOKAS, Norway's cash-handling service.
> Behind these walls, millions of kroner moved every day. They were counted, sorted, sealed and sent out to banks across the region.
> The building sat in the middle of town, by the cathedral, the shops and a busy pedestrian street.
> Hiding in plain sight was its security. Nobody would dare rob a vault in the middle of the city.

**VISUAL:** Inside, we follow a cash cart past a thick window that looks out on a back courtyard. Hold on the glass. It is greenish and layered, and it reflects the room.

**NARRATOR:**
> The building's real defence wasn't a gun or a guard.
> It was glass. Armoured, layered, and built to take punishment.
> Remember this window.

## CHAPTER 2 — THE ARCHITECT

> 🟦 **2D ANIMATION** · 2:00 – 3:30 · *(1:30)*

**VISUAL:** Shift to Oslo, in a colder palette. A silhouette at a kitchen table covered in maps, notebooks and a stopwatch.

**NARRATOR:**
> Five hundred kilometres east, in Oslo, a man was making a plan.
> His name was David Toska.
> He was not the kind of criminal who kicks in a door and hopes for the best. Toska was a planner.
> He thought in timelines, routes and minutes.

**VISUAL:** Toska's notebook pages animate. Arrows, clocks and the outline of a building appear, and in the centre a rectangle labelled *VINDU*, Norwegian for "window".

**NARRATOR:**
> His target was the NOKAS depot in Stavanger.
> The money was guarded by a building, and a building can be beaten.
> The police, however, can always come. Unless they can't.

**VISUAL:** The notebook splits into two columns: **GET IN** and **KEEP THEM OUT**.

**NARRATOR:**
> So the plan had two halves.
> One team would break into the depot.
> The other team's job was to freeze an entire city's police force in place, for just long enough.

**VISUAL:** Masked silhouettes step forward one by one, each tagged with a role: *DRIVER, BLOCKER, SPOTTER, GUNMAN, SLEDGEHAMMER.*

**NARRATOR:**
> He built a crew of more than a dozen men.
> Among them was Erling Havnå, later known as *the sledgehammer man*.
> There was Metkel Betew, one of the central figures.
> And there was Kjell Alrich Schumann, a name that would come to matter more than any other in this story.

**MUSIC:** A low pulse starts, like a heartbeat.

## CHAPTER 3 — STRIKE ONE, STRIKE TWO

> 🟦 **2D ANIMATION** · 3:30 – 3:50 · *(0:20)*

**VISUAL:** Toska's notebook. The *VINDU* rectangle is circled again and again until the pen tears the paper.

**NARRATOR:**
> Here is the part most people don't know.
> The robbery on the fifth of April was not the first attempt. It was the third.

**TRANSITION:** The tear in the paper becomes a crack, the crack becomes glass, and the glass shatters into depth.

> 🟦 **2D ANIMATION** · 4:20 – 5:15 · *(0:55)*

**VISUAL:** Toska's notebook with one tally mark. Then a second one appears.

**NARRATOR:**
> The armoured glass was far stronger than Toska had calculated.
> A plan built to the minute… stopped by a pane of glass.
> Most crews would have walked away. Toska went back to the drawing board. More force. More time.

**VISUAL:** Late winter. Headlights wind through dark roads out of the city into a forest marked **SØRMARKA**.

**NARRATOR:**
> All through the late winter of 2004, the gang test-drove their escape route over and over, from the city centre out to the woods at Sørmarka.
> By April, they knew Stavanger's streets better than the people whose job was to protect them.

**VISUAL:** In Toska's notebook, a third tally mark appears beside *VINDU*.

## CHAPTER 4 — 5 APRIL 2004

> 🟦 **2D ANIMATION** · 5:15 – 6:15 · *(1:00)*

**VISUAL:** Title flash: **MONDAY, 5 APRIL 2004 · 03:00**. Darkness. Vans and trucks move into position in silence.

**NARRATOR:**
> In the early hours of Monday the fifth of April, the pieces moved onto the board.
> Weapons, spike strips and smoke grenades were all accounted for.

**VISUAL:** Clock: **07:59**. Ordinary life. A man with a coffee, a cyclist, shop shutters rolling up on the cathedral square.

**NARRATOR:**
> Stavanger is waking up. None of them know they are standing on the board.

**MUSIC:** It drops to near silence. Just a ticking clock.

**VISUAL:** **08:00.** The police HQ on Lagårdsveien. A white truck rolls down the garage ramp, stops across the exit and erupts in flames. Tear-gas canisters bounce across the front steps. A spike strip unrolls across an empty street.

**NARRATOR:**
> Front one: the police station.
> A truck is set on fire across the garage exit. Tear gas is thrown at the main entrance. Spikes are laid across the roads.
> In a few moments, Stavanger's police are trapped behind their own doors.

**VISUAL:** Smash cut to the NOKAS courtyard. A masked man lifts a sledgehammer.

**NARRATOR:**
> Front two: the window. Third time.

**TRANSITION:** The sledgehammer hits the 2D glass, and the frame shatters into 3D.

---

> 🟥 **3D CINEMATIC 🎬 2 — "113"** · 6:15 – 6:45 · *(0:30)*

*Mood: relentless. The climax of the robbery, and the moment the glass finally breaks.*

| Time in scene | Shot | Camera | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:08 | The NOKAS courtyard in morning light: the same window as in 🎬 1. A battering ram slams into it, and the glass bows inward. | The same angle as in 🎬 1, from inside, but in daylight. | *THUNK… THUNK…* |
| 0:08–0:20 | Gunfire into the glass. Each round adds a white star until the window is a galaxy of fractures. A counter is burned into the corner: 20… 57… 89… **113**. | Locked-off close-up from inside the counting room, with each muzzle flash lighting the room. | Rhythmic automatic fire, filtered through the glass. |
| 0:20–0:30 | The window gives. It does not shatter. It *sags* inward like wet paper. A gloved hand reaches through. | Slow motion, with the camera pulling back into the counting room. | A sickening crunch, then silence. |

**NARRATOR** *(over 0:20–0:30)*:
> One hundred and thirteen shots. And this time… the glass gives.

> ⬛ **END 3D, BACK TO 2D:** the gloved hand comes through the hole, and its reflection in a shard flattens into a 2D frame.

---

> 🟦 **2D ANIMATION** · 6:45 – 7:45 · *(1:00)*

**VISUAL:** Inside the counting room, from a low angle. Staff are pressed against the walls, frightened faces lit by flashes. Masked figures climb through the hole.

**NARRATOR:**
> Inside, NOKAS employees watch armed men climb into their workplace.
> They are not soldiers. They are people who came to work on a Monday morning.

**VISUAL:** Bags are filled and passed hand to hand out through the broken window. Banknotes flutter in the air.

**NARRATOR:**
> The robbers demand the money, and they get it: bag after bag, more than **57 million kroner**.
> At the time, it was the biggest robbery in Norwegian history.
> But outside, the plan was already starting to come apart.

## CHAPTER 5 — THE OFFICER

> 🟦 **2D ANIMATION** · 7:45 – 8:15 · *(0:30)*

**NARRATOR:**

**NARRATOR** *(slowly)*:
> One of the bullets struck Arne Sigve Klungland in the head.
> He died there, in the middle of his own city, on a Monday morning.

## CHAPTER 6 — INTO THE WOODS

> 🟦 **2D ANIMATION** · 9:05 – 9:45 · *(0:40)*

**MUSIC:** The pulse returns, faster now.

**VISUAL:** A top-down map of Stavanger. Red lines race from the city centre outward along the rehearsed route, then vanish one by one into the dark green forest of Sørmarka.

**NARRATOR:**
> The robbers loaded the money and ran, along the route they had driven all winter, into the forest at Sørmarka.
> Vehicles were swapped and evidence was burned. Within minutes, the men in black dissolved into the trees.
> Toska had planned to beat a window, a police station and a city.
> He had not planned to kill a police officer. And from this moment, Norway would not rest until every man was found.

**NARRATOR:**

## EPILOGUE — WHAT THE GLASS KEPT

> 🟦 **2D ANIMATION** · 12:15 – 13:00 · *(0:45)*

> More than fifty-seven million kroner was taken that morning.

## Production notes

**Runtime:** about 13:00, with 11:00 of 🟦 2D and 2:00 of 🟥 3D (4 × 30 seconds). The narration is about 1,150 words. The rest of the time goes to pauses, music beats and the 3D sequences, which are almost silent.

**3D production priorities**, from the most to the least demanding:
1. **🎬 2 "113"** has slow-motion glass simulation, muzzle flashes and the climax of the film. Budget it first.
2. **🎬 4 "One Year to the Day"** needs an exterior seafront environment, a small crowd and vehicles.
3. **🎬 1 "The Window Wins"** needs one courtyard set, rain and glass simulation. The courtyard and window are **reused in 🎬 2**, relit for daylight.
4. **🎬 3 "The Windshield"** needs one vehicle interior and one street. It is the simplest in polygons but the hardest in tone. Test it with an audience before final render.

**Shared 3D assets:** the NOKAS courtyard and window (🎬 1 and 2), the glass-crack shader (all four), the masked robber rig (🎬 1, 2 and 3), the police vehicle (🎬 3) and the cathedral spire (🎬 3).

**Recurring motifs (the red thread):**
1. **The glass.** It appears in the opening crack, the courtyard window, the windshield, the car window in Málaga and the reflection in the epilogue. Glass is also the doorway every time the film passes between 🟦 2D and 🟥 3D.
2. **The clock.** It starts at 03:00 and 08:00 on 5 April 2004, and it closes on 5 April 2005.
3. **The hammer sound.** It is the first sound of the film, it hits the window in 🎬 1 and 🎬 2, and it returns as the car door in 🎬 4.

**Fact-check before publishing.** These points were confirmed in search results:
- The date (5 April 2004, about 08:00) and the place (NOKAS counting centre, Kongens gate, Stavanger city centre).
- The police HQ on Lagårdsveien was blocked by a burning truck at the garage exit, with tear gas at the entrance and spike strips on the roads.
- Entry was through the courtyard window, using a sledgehammer and a battering ram, with 113 shots fired into it.
- The robbery was the third attempt, and the glass had beaten an earlier one.
- The escape route to Sørmarka was test-driven in late winter 2004.
- About 57.4 million NOK was stolen. About 6 million has been accounted for, and about 51 million was never recovered.
- Police lieutenant Arne Sigve Klungland was killed by a shot to the head while in a police command vehicle.
- Thirteen men were convicted on 10 March 2006, with 181 years in total.
- Kjell Alrich Schumann admitted firing the fatal shots during the Gulating appeal in September 2006.
- Toska confessed on 12 April 2005 but denied shooting Klungland. He fled to Spain with his partner and their one-year-old son, and was arrested in Málaga on 5 April 2005 after surveillance and email tracing.
- Erling Havnå was known as "the sledgehammer man" (*sleggemannen*), and Metkel Betew was among those convicted.

**Dramatised details in the 3D sequences.** These are staging choices, not documented facts:
- 🎬 1: the rain, the night and the exact way the earlier attempt played out. It is documented only that the glass defeated an earlier attempt.
- 🎬 2: the exact choreography and the slow-motion counter.
- 🎬 3: the route and the windshield breaking. The documented facts are only that he was in a police command vehicle and was shot in the head.
- 🎬 4: the coffee cup, the calendar and the arrest choreography. The documented facts are only the arrest in Málaga on 5 April 2005, after surveillance and email tracing.

**Verify or soften these points before publishing:**
- *The exact number of robbers at the scene.* The script says "more than a dozen" to stay safe.
- *Toska's final sentence.* Sources disagree between 18, 19 and 21 years, so the script deliberately gives no figure.
- *The link to the theft of The Scream.* The script presents it only as a theory that was never proven.
- *"Biggest robbery in Norwegian history".* This was true at the time, so keep the words "at the time".
- *The family in the final line.* Check before naming any relatives of Klungland.
